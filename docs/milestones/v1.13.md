### A low-angle shift on magnetic lines no nuclear line can check (2056, closed 2026-11-09)

On constant-wavelength neutron data the lowest lines are often magnetic, and they sit where
axial asymmetry is largest. In that case no nuclear line in the sample says how much
asymmetry the instrument has, so whatever is assumed lands on the moment. The Ba₂FeSbSe₅
protocol recorded that by hand, as −50 mdeg at 9.35° held as a systematic. The fit report now
says it itself. `LOW_ANGLE_ASYMMETRY_UNCALIBRATED` (info) fires when a seen magnetic-only line
lies below every seen nuclear-only line. It quotes each such line's position shift (the best-fit rigid shift of the drawn profile, a weighted
least-squares regression on the line's own position derivative and profile, with the
counting-noise esd), the first nuclear line's shift, and what a cot 2θ shape through the
magnetic lines predicts there. It decides nothing. Measured: Ba₂FeSbSe₅ G4.1 at 1.5 K,
−51.3(2.2) mdeg at 9.38° against −5.1(2.1) at the nuclear 21.67°; LaMnO₃ BT-1 at 50 K,
+19.6(1.8) mdeg at 11.53°. The two samples need opposite treatment (#1090), so the
adopt-or-hold comparison ships as a skill recipe. The opt-in Bérar–Baldinozzi term and a
structured field for the shifts went to #1499, with the scope ruling the WP never received.
