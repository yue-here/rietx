**[1074](../wp/1074-restraint-weight-schedule.md) closed 2026-08-16: the last
of the McCusker compliance set, and a fit that converges to a 4.8 Å bond.**
Gap 6, and the second of the two 1.0.x members. The outgoing focus below was
written when it was the only one left; with it closed the set is complete and
[1003](../wp/1003-api-freeze-pypi.md)'s sequencing question has no open
recommendation behind it.

The mechanism is one scalar. `Stage.restraint_weight_scale` is c_w of eq (7),
S = S_y + c_w·S_G, frozen onto the compiled model with the hkl list — the
paper's schedule ("set high at the beginning … then reduced … as the structural
model improves") changes it between stages and never inside one, which is the
frozen-per-stage rule applied as designed rather than excepted. 1.0 is the
identity, measured: a restrained five-stage rutile fit is bit-identical to main
in Rwp, GoF, χ², the restrained coordinate, its esd, the cell and the restraint
deviation, to the last bit of the hex float.

**Demonstrating it needed a case the data genuinely under-determines, and two
synthetic attempts failed for a reason worth keeping.** In P1 the inverted
structure is an *exact* powder degeneracy — |F(h)|² is unchanged by x → −x —
and it preserves every interatomic distance, so restraints cannot separate the
two at any weight and a wrong-basin fit there says nothing about c_w. A
centrosymmetric group is its own inverse. In P -1 with six coordinate DOFs on
two weak oxygens against 35 reflections, from a start whose Zr–O is 3.73 Å for a
1.87 Å bond: at a flat c_w = 1 the fit **converges** with that distance at
**4.834 Å**, the restraint 148σ in tension, coordinates 0.425 rms from truth;
at c_w = 300 then 1 the stiff stage lands the bond at 1.866 Å (0.03σ) and the
relaxed stage converges at 1.872 Å, 0.00107 rms. Same plan shape, same data,
same start.

**The failed fit's own statistics are the weaker channel**, which is the Rwp
invariant paying out on a case where it would have been tempting to use it:
Rwp 0.0393 against 0.0327, GoF 1.23 against 1.02, and a difference curve inside
±3σ but for three excursions. A reader watching Rwp sees a slightly worse fit;
a reader watching `result.restraints` sees a 4.8 Å bond. And a schedule buys a
*path*, not an endpoint — where the relaxed stage's basin is reachable from
both starts, its answer is the flat plan's answer.

**Two findings one rank down.** The seam is `model/restraints.py`'s second
consumer: `model/geometry.py` calls `restraint_partials` at σ = weight = 1
precisely to get *unweighted* partials, and every reported geometry esd is
built from them, so the scalar is applied at the row build and never in the
shared function. 1072's mailbox named the geometry Monte Carlo as the assertion
that would catch a leak; injecting both bugs says otherwise, and the division
is now recorded in both tests — that fixture declares no restraints and runs at
c_w = 1, so a leak *conditioned on the model* is invisible to it, while an
unconditional factor in `pref` is invisible to the new pin. Second, the fast
suite caught the `.rxt` grammar: renderer and parser each carried the same
hard-coded stage-key tuple, so a new `StageSpec` field would have been dropped
on every save rather than merely unrendered — `strain_seed`'s own loss, one
rank down. The keys are derived from `StageSpec.model_fields` now.

**[1073](../wp/1073-capillary-displacement.md) closed 2026-08-15: the capillary
gets the position correction it was missing, and the dataset it was supposed to
prove it on says not to refine it.** Gap 7, and the first of the two 1.0.x
members of the set. The outgoing focus below was written when 1073 and 1074
were both open.

The correction itself is small — one shift function, two parameters, one branch
on each forward path — and the analytic chain took it for free, because a
parameter that moves only the peak position already had a column builder. What
took the work was that **neither of the WP's two premises survived being
measured**, and both were premises about how one would *know* the correction
works.

**The sign convention.** McCusker prints eq (4) as (x·sin2θ − y·cos2θ)/R and
draws no axes; GSAS-II pairs the letter x with the *cos 2θ* term instead. The
letters therefore carry nothing, and the package derives the expression instead:
to first order the apparent angle at the goniometer centre moves by (d·t̂)/R with
t̂ = (−sin2θ, cos2θ), which equals eq (4) under x = −a, y = −b — a statement
about the two expressions, not about the paper's axes, which it never draws. The
test is an exact ray-circle intersection, sharing no constant with the
implementation, asserting agreement to second order **and** that the agreement
is not exact — because a linearisation that matched to machine precision would
not be a linearisation. This is the "document conventions by physics, not
letters" rule meeting a case where the letters are actively wrong.

**The null test is a degeneracy test.** The plan was that 11-BM, a
crystal-analyser instrument the paper says has no displacement error, should
recover x, y ≈ 0. It does not. Over NAC's certified 2-24° the trio
{1, sin2θ, cos2θ} has a unit-column Gram eigenvalue of 1.6e-5, so the fit rides
the null direction to a bound: +1.000 mm (the parameter's max) ± 2.78 and
+0.72 ± 1.01, ρ(zero, across) = −1.0000, two eigenvalue-zero soft modes,
`BOUND_HIT`. **Rwp improves** — 0.14025 → 0.13843 — while the cell leaves its
acceptance band, 10.25121 → 10.23976 Å, 1117 ppm and 5.6× the tolerance. On
11-BM LaB6 660a over 2-60° the offsets come back +0.544 ± 0.054 and
+0.527 ± 0.041 mm, ten sigma from zero, with a moving −658 ppm and Rwp falling
0.0885 → 0.0837. Both are 2θ-short at 0.41 Å, and that is the whole mechanism.

Two things generalise. First, **"this instrument does not have that error" is
not the same claim as "refine it and get zero"** — the second needs the data to
be able to measure it, and a paper's statement about optics says nothing about
conditioning. Second, this is the v0.5 method result in its sharpest form yet: a
session judging this correction by Δ Rwp would have accepted it on both real
datasets and shipped a cell wrong in the fourth digit. The channel that told the
truth was the esds, the correlations and `BOUND_HIT`.

**And the endpoint hides the cause.** On a synthetic capillary carrying a
0.30/−0.20 mm offset, denying the correction does not leave a cos-2θ signature
to find: the zero shift and the cell imitate most of it, the cell comes back
290 ppm out, and the converged report suggests **no position action at all**.
The `zero` stage's own rung names `refine_capillary_offset_along_beam` at 0.66
confidence. That is WP-1058's rule — a plan absorbs an error it cannot free into
whatever it can, and converges suggesting nothing — with a case that was
generated rather than found.

A latent defect fell out on the way: `_POSITION_ACTIONS` was geometry-blind, so
a capillary fit whose peaks followed cos θ was told to refine
`sample_displacement`, a parameter `ParameterTable` force-fixes outside
Bragg-Brentano. Both the template set and the action map are now keyed by
`Geometry.kind`, which is `THRESHOLDS_VERSION` 0.9 → 1.0 and the freeze's to
ratify — 1003's mailbox carries it, including the one row deliberately left
alone (`flat_plate_transmission`, whose diagnosis is at least the right one).

**[1072](../wp/1072-geometry-table.md) closed 2026-08-15: the structure's own
numbers, with esds that mean what §10 asks them to mean.** Gap 4 of the
compliance audit, and the last of the set recommended before the freeze — the
outgoing focus below was written when three had landed.

McCusker §11 ranks the chemical sense of the structure *with* the profile fit
and above every R value, and until now a converged result said nothing about it:
`RefinementResult.geometry` is a `GeometryTable` of distances (bonding and
nonbonding, the paper's own split) and angles, and `write_refinement_cif`
writes them as `_geom_bond` / `_geom_contact` / `_geom_angle` loops with a
`_space_group_symop_operation_xyz` loop beside them — a `site_symmetry` code is
an index into a listed order and points nowhere without one.

**§10's rule, made measurable.** The esds go through the whole parameter
covariance, and the diagonal-only number is carried beside each one, the
`qpa.weight_fractions` precedent. On 11-BM NAC refined with
`mccusker_structural` (Rwp 8.17 %, GoF 3.10) the full/diagonal ratio runs
**0.713 to 1.152 over 88 distances** and **0.640 to 1.204 over 79 angles**,
median 1.00 and 1.03. The spread's *direction* is the finding: dropping the
correlations is not a conservative approximation, it is wrong either way, and
on this pattern it would understate one esd by 15 % and overstate another by
29 %. The refined geometry itself sits within 1.3σ of the deposited structure
throughout — Al–F 1.8055(159) and 1.7788(136) Å against 1.8244 and 1.7833 —
which is what makes those ratios readable as a statement about the esd rather
than about the fit.

**Two things the implementation is built on, both cheaper than they look.** A
geometry row *is* a restraint row with σ = weight = 1, so the derivative chain
through the coordinate DOFs' affine ties and the six cell parameters is
`model/restraints.py`'s, not a second copy; and the neighbour search runs over
the orbit ops `PhaseSites` already froze, so the table and the structure
factors count the same orbits. The whole single-phase NAC table costs 4 ms.

**What proved it, and what did not.** Every distance-value test passed against
three published structures while a same-site deduplication was quietly dropping
real neighbours — the map it assumed was an involution is not one when the site
symmetry is non-trivial. **Orbit counting** caught it: |A_ij|·m_i = |A_ji|·m_j
counts every i–j bond in the cell twice by two routes, and no partial search
satisfies it by accident. The listing rule that survived is the plain one —
every asymmetric-unit atom's whole environment, so the row count naming an atom
*is* its coordination number — with the CIF's one-direction convention restored
in the exporter, where the audience is a parser rather than a chemist. Separately,
a loop variable named `theta` for an angle in degrees shadowed the parameter
vector, so from the second phase on the covariance call received a bond angle;
every geometry test was single-phase and real two-phase data raised on the
first run.

**[1071](../wp/1071-data-support-checks.md) closed 2026-08-15: the package can
now say how many observations it actually has.** Gaps 3 and 9 of the compliance
audit. The outgoing focus below was written when four of the set were open;
three are now closed and the freeze's sequencing question is down to three.

The number the least-squares algorithm calls N is the number of profile steps,
and §9's whole warning is that refining against it outruns the data in silence.
On 11-BM NAC that is **22 003 points against 132 reflections** — a factor of
167, and nothing on `Statistics` said so. `RefinementResult.data_support` now
carries the raw unique-reflection count, the overlap-corrected count of
Altomare *et al.* (1995), the structural half of the parameter split, and both
ratios. It gates nothing, which is the standing thesis and also the honest
posture: the estimate is one the paper itself says "may not have a rigorous
basis".

**The two corroborations worth keeping.** Altomare measured M_ind/M between
0.22 and 0.50 across twenty real structures; this implementation lands at
**0.45 on 11-BM NAC and 0.43 on the GSAS-II fluorapatite**, which is the only
external check on the estimator available without their data. And §2's sampling
rule, measured on the same three protocols: 11-BM NAC 9.32 steps per FWHM,
SRM 660c 9.55, FAP 4.71. The two reference-grade datasets sit just inside
"generally not more than ten", where a well-collected pattern should be, and
the one that trips the flag is a lab tutorial dataset — at the boundary rather
than far past it. A rule that fires on nothing and a rule that fires on
everything are both useless; this one fires on one of three.

**The finding is that the sampling metric inverts without a prominence floor,
on exactly the patterns it exists to judge.** On an undersampled synthetic
(17 reflections, 3.2 steps per FWHM by construction) plain 5σ peak detection
returns 49 peaks and a median width of 1.38 steps: Poisson noise on a
10⁵-count peak puts several 5σ maxima across its own jagged top, so the metric
reports catastrophic undersampling on a pattern that is merely *noisy*, and it
does so most confidently where the counting statistics are best. The floor
separates a maximum from a bump; with it the measurement tracks the width the
pattern was generated with to 3-4 % across a 10× sweep, and 5σ and 20σ agree
exactly — which is the guard that it is a floor and not a tuning.

The one deviation from Altomare §2(d), taken for cost, is in the WP: measured
at +0.00 % to +0.74 % against the estimator's own α = 2 → α = 4 spread of 6.5 %,
buying 923 ms → 14 ms and quadratic → linear scaling. **The general shape:
re-shaping a published estimator is defensible when the deviation is measured
against the estimator's own stated precision rather than against zero.**

**[1070](../wp/1070-user-facing-constraints.md) closed 2026-08-15: the package
can now say "these are one parameter".** Gap 2 of the compliance audit, and its
largest: McCusker asks for constraints four times (§7 twice, §12.7 ix, §12.8 v)
and this package had only restraints. The outgoing focus below was written when
five of the set were open; two are now closed and the freeze's sequencing
question is down to four.

The verb was the cheap half — `AffineTie` and `ParameterTable.set_tie` already
carried the crystal-system cell ties and the Wyckoff coordinate ties, so
`tie`/`tie_equal`/`untie` are a surface plus a `set_tie` node plus
`RefinementState.ties`, which a checkout has to restore because a tie is *not* a
property of the models: every `ParameterTable` build rederives the symmetry ties
from the space group and knows nothing about a user's.

**The finding is one rank down, in the Jacobian.** `_make_jacobian` dispatches
on the free path's own *name*, and each branch computes only the rows it was
written for — one background design row, one atom's x/y/z read off C, the phases
named in the path's own prefix. That is exact while the only ties are the
derived ones, which never reach outside the branch that handles them: a cubic
`b`←`a` stays inside phase 0, a Wyckoff coordinate inside its atom. A user tie
reaches further, and it fails *silently* — the column comes back short, nothing
raises. So each branch now declares the reach it covers, `_column_extras` reads
what each column actually moves off C, and anything further falls to the
whole-model FD fallback, which is exact because it decodes through C like the
residual does. Measured: with `background.c1` tied to `c0` the un-gated branch
is wrong by 49 % of the column's own scale.

The general lesson is not about ties. **A dispatch keyed on a name is a claim
about what that name reaches**, and it stays true only as long as nothing new
can widen the reach. The affine block was built in WP-0301 precisely so that
one parameter could stand for several; the Jacobian's branches were written as
if it could not.

Second: the tests assert **additivity** (a tied column is the sum of the columns
each dependent would have alone) rather than agreement with finite differences,
because an FD reference cannot separate a gated column from an un-gated one
where the gate's own answer *is* that finite difference. When the fallback is
FD, FD is not a test of it.

The §7 story measured on fluorapatite's three phosphate oxygens is in the WP's
handover and in `docs/VALIDATION.md`: 20 → 18 free parameters, esd 0.1282 →
0.0899 Å², and Rwp moving by 0.05 % of itself — which is the invariant paying
out again. Rwp could not have refereed this question in either direction.

**[1069](../wp/1069-structure-r-factors.md) closed 2026-08-15: the first of the
McCusker compliance set, and the release now quotes a structure R.** The
outgoing focus below was written when the set had six open WPs and the freeze's
one sequencing question was which of them land first; 1069 was the
release-credibility half of that recommendation — every published Rietveld
refinement quotes at least one structure R, and the pdCIF export had no
structure-R tag at all.

What it cost was small because the machinery existed: the partition
`lebail_update` performs, run once at the converged values with the shares
taken from the *structural model* instead of from empirical intensities. Its
one real design decision is that the observed and calculated counts of a
reflection differ only by their ratio, so every correction — scale, preferred
orientation, Lp, extinction, absorption, roughness, the emission-line weights —
cancels, and a new correction costs the R factors nothing. The measured values,
the tag lookups, and why the FAP `.EXP` comparison could not be made are in the
WP's handover.

The finding that outlived it: **R_B is unweighted, so a trace phase's value is
not comparable with the major phase's.** On 11-BM NAC the 1.35 wt % CaF₂
impurity reads 0.385 against NAC's 0.052, and the whole of that is four
reflections at I(obs)/I(calc) ≈ 2.2, each under a strong NAC peak. Two
mechanisms and no way to separate them here — eq (14) carries no w, and the
partition hands out counts the *major* phase failed to describe. That is what
Cox & Papoular's weighted variant answers, and it stayed out of scope.

**[1046](../wp/1046-candidate-cap-before-ranking.md) closed 2026-08-09: the cap
was the ranking, and the ranking was ignoring the one thing the gate believes.**
`SearchSpec.max_candidates` described itself as "a cap, not a ranking: the panel
ranks, and 1024 consensus-merges". It was applied by every (engine × system)
unit *before* the merge, and Borda is a rank-sum over the pool being ranked — so
each unit's ordering was a function of what else that unit happened to find, and
a **longer** search could lose an answer a shorter one returned first. Measured
on bethanechol set F: the published lattice is rank 1 at a 5 s and 15 s budget
and **absent from the result** at 30 s and 60 s, repeats at one budget agreeing
exactly. It is now the *reported* cap, applied once, by consensus; a unit hands
the merge `ENGINE_POOL_MULTIPLE ×` it. The two numbers exist because they price
different things — the pool is panel time, the reported cap is Le Bail
validation — and `INDEX_CANDIDATES_TRUNCATED` states what the answer left out.

**Raising the pool was not enough, and the second truncation is the more
interesting one.** `rank_candidates` keeps `4 × max_candidates` for the panel
under a cheap pre-rank (most lines indexed, then smallest volume), and consensus
inherited that multiplier — so the number the answer *reports* also decided how
far down the merge was scored at all: 48 of a 260-lattice merge. The pre-rank's
stated conservatism does not transfer to that layer either. It is safe against a
candidate's *own* supercell; it is not safe against a hundred unrelated
low-symmetry cells that index more lines than the truth does. Consensus now
scores the whole merge, which it can afford because what reaches it is already
bounded by the pool.

**Then the half the WP said could not be skipped, and it did not need a new
figure of merit.** With the pool open, set F entered at rank 3 and set Db was
*displaced* from rank 1 — WP-1026 had measured the same trade at cap 60 and
recorded it as net zero. The mechanism is not Borda's pool dependence: on Db the
`svd`-only candidate that takes first place beats the truth on the panel three
members to two, so no re-weighting reaches it (WP-1041 measured and refuted
those aggregates, and every one of its refutations stands). What separates them
is **who found the lattice** — the quantity the confidence gate already treats as
categorical, since `grade` floors a candidate with fewer than two finders at
`low` before a single caveat is read. The list was ordering candidates the gate
refuses to promote above the one it could: on the GSAS-II fluorapatite pattern
searched over all seven systems, the six candidates above the truth were
`trial_error`-only and the truth was all three engines'.

**And the first form of that key was wrong, which the benchmark's other half
caught.** Ranking on the *number* of engines looks like the graded version of the
same statement, and it is not: the engines' reach differs by system, so all three
meet in a cheap orthorhombic domain while only two ever reach an expensive

**[1047](../wp/1047-vendor-pattern-formats.md) opened 2026-08-08: the seam

`xy` was the total fallback (`matches=lambda p: True`), so opening a Bruker

tightening.

A `.chi`'s fourth header line is `<npoints> [<ndatasets>]`, and `2000 1` is a
perfectly good pair of floats, so the fallback appended it as a point at
x = 2000 — a phantom datum thousands of degrees outside the pattern, surviving
every plot and quietly widening the fitted range. The regression test
demonstrates it both ways: the new reader returns three points where `read_xy`
on the same bytes returns four. Its `matches` is two gates, and the second —
`npoints` against the rows that follow — is the package's **one stated
exemption** to the bounded-head rule: O(N), behind a cheap shape gate, and the
only thing that separates this format from the catch-all. A `.dif` peak list
is now recognised *in order to be refused*, through a `PatternFormat.refuses`
field rather than a side table, so `reader_formats` does not come to mean two
things.

Two rules came out of the seam itself. **A reader may repair a stranger's file
only where it can say that it did**: `read_pattern` grew
`structure_from_cif`'s `diagnostics=` channel — returning a bare `PatternData`
was an accident, not a design — and `ascending()` applies the
report-vs-contradiction test to 2θ order in one place. Descending is reversed
and an equal-y duplicate dropped, both reported; a duplicate with *differing*
y and a non-monotone range **raise**, because averaging invents a datum,
dropping picks one, and concatenating stitched ranges silently mixes two step
sizes and two counting times into one weighting regime. GSAS-II concatenates.
And **one option vocabulary, two levels**: `READER_OPTIONS` build-wide,
`PatternFormat.options` per format, `reader_options_for` the single authority
— which is exactly what lets a typo raise while an option this format does not
take is dropped *and reported*, a distinction no single list can make.

The method note worth keeping: the truncation harness
(`tests/test_readers_robust.py`, twenty cuts per real fixture) **found a defect
on its first run** — a truncated FXYE failed as "cannot reshape array of size
527 into shape (3)", a true statement about an array and useless to anyone.
The invariant it exists to protect is for the six container parsers still to
come, and it caught the one reader that was already there.

**1047 continued 2026-08-09 with the first two vendor formats, and both of them

  frozen window, the solver's only escape is to drive the scale to zero, and it
  reports `converged` at Rwp 0.99999. A bar at 1.0 would have missed the
  reported defect by 1e-5.
- The `r` fix asked for an audit of "other softplus `min=0.0` parameters with
  the same reachable-zero bug". Fourteen declare it and thirteen are fine, for
  a reason worth more than the list: everywhere else **zero is the identity**

  is interior at 1 with a pole at the bound. The pattern to watch is not
  "softplus with `min=0`" but "softplus with `min=0` *and* a pole at zero".

**[1044](../wp/1044-gui-view-cursor-theme.md) closed 2026-08-06: four defects
reported from use, and the two that read as unrelated were one sentence of
plotly's autorange.**

The report was four lines: the theme does not survive loading a project;
horizontal zoom does not work when there are excluded regions; the armed
range/exclude gesture shows the zoom cursor; selecting a peak resets the zoom.
The first three name a cause and only one of them is right.

**"Excluded regions break the zoom" is true and is not about regions.** Every
redraw handed `Plotly.react` a layout with no `range`, so plotly re-autoranged
over everything drawn — and what is drawn is not only the fetched window. The
peak markers span the whole pattern, because the peak list is not windowed, and
so do the mask shapes, which are `xref: "x"` and therefore take part in the
autorange. That last property is *WP-1033's own finding*, recorded there as the
reason `maskShapes` clips to the measured extent (bands drawn past the data
"became the range", −40 to 100 on a 0.5–59.99° pattern); what the clip could not
do is stop them expanding a range that had been zoomed *inside* the extent.
Measured in Chrome on the synthetic fixture, a drag to 9.97–14.66° came back:

| also on the plot             | axis after the refetch |
|------------------------------|------------------------|
| nothing                      | 9.97–14.66 ✓           |
| a peak list                  | 4.57–24.85             |
| an excluded region at 4–5°   | 3.99–24.88             |
| an excluded region at 20–21° | 4.57–24.85             |
| a fitted range of 8–18°      | 3.00–24.94             |

So the zoom worked on an empty plot and nowhere else, and the reporter had
attributed it to the change they had just made. The same react is what threw the
view away on a peak edit — the fourth line — and on the **raw** view (peaks, no
fit) there is not even a window fetch to land back in, so a shift-click went
9.97–14.66 → the full 1.74–25.25 in one gesture.

The repair is a rule this codebase already had, one panel over:
[1015](../wp/1015-structure-viewer.md)'s **`react` rebuilds the scene, so the
view must be handed back on every draw**, read back immediately before the call
rather than tracked in a variable — there it was the gl3d camera, here it is the
axis ranges, and `autorange === false` is exactly "the user has said" (plotly
sets it on a drag and puts it back on a double-click). Handing the *window* back
turned out to have three consequences the browser had to be asked about, none of
which reading the code would have produced. `doubleClick` had to become
`"autosize"`, because plotly's default `reset+autosize` means "back to the range
the plot was drawn with" — which is now the zoom itself, so the gesture became a
no-op. `view()` had to compare the knobs **untracked**, or the fetch effect took
a dependency on the residual selector and choosing Δ over Δ/σ asked the server
for the window again — caught by making the jsdom suite *count the reacts one
click costs*, which is the assertion that generalises. And counting them found a
third thing nobody had reported: the repaint effect **tracked** `held`, so every
fetch cost a second identical react — 2 per zoom drag and 6 at boot, at WP-1032's
measured ~111 ms each.

**The cursor is one line of plotly and worth stating as a rule.**
`Cartesian.updateFx` gives the drag layer `cursor-crosshair` for every dragmode
that is not `pan`, so `dragmode: "select"` and `dragmode: "zoom"` are
pointer-identical: arming changed what a drag meant and nothing under the pointer
said so. `col-resize` goes on the plot-area **rect** rather than on the layer
plotly styles, because an inherited cursor loses to any direct declaration — no
specificity fight with a stylesheet the app does not own.

**And the theme was a scope question, not a bug.** [1029](../wp/1029-gui-usability.md)
persisted the choice in `ProjectDoc.ui` "like every other GUI setting", so
`readUi` re-reads it off whichever document is open — and a project that has
never been told resets it. Measured: choose dark, open a second project, back to
`system`. The line between the two scopes is what a key is *about*: a column
width or Simple/Advanced is a fact about a project (four phases, so the table
wants to be wide), a theme is a fact about the person and the room they are in.
So `GET`/`POST /api/settings` opens an app-level `ui` dict in the state directory
beside `recent.json`, with `POST /api/project`'s exact grammar — top-level merge,
`null` drops a key, persisted on the verb — and the theme survives the project,
the `--port` and the browser profile. Two things fell out for free. The control
left the `{#if project}` block, so the empty state — the screen a first-time user
starts on — can be made dark. And the choice is now settable **while a run is in
flight**, which is precisely the finding 1029 recorded and could not fix from
inside a route it did not own: a `ui` patch was 409'd with "this verb would
change the model a compiled stage was built from", a sentence that was never true
of a theme. The project's other four `ui` keys still ride that route, so 1003's
inherited freeze question is smaller rather than answered.

Method note, because it decided the session: the first three measurements were
all made against the **wrong view**. The fixture project's fit lives in memory,
so a freshly opened `.rex` has no result and the plot draws the *raw* pattern —
where the relayout handler returns early, nothing refetches, and every zoom
"works". A round of five mask configurations all came back green before a network
count showed zero `/api/result/window` fetches at boot. **Count the requests
before believing the pixels**, which is the same instinct as 1015's "when a claim
is about an event, count the events".

**[1016](../wp/1016-sequential-series-panel.md) closed 2026-08-05: an in-situ ramp

WP-1008's `### Inherited` said, in as many words, that "`SequentialRefinement`
already emits per-pattern events and takes `events=`/`cancel=`". It took neither:
`_fit_one` called `ref.fit(data, mode=, plan=, two_theta_limits=)` and stopped

panel you can debug afterwards. **An inherited claim is not a measurement** — the
inverse of the lesson the previous four sessions kept learning, and worth the same
weight.

So the library half went first. `fit` takes both; `_SeriesStream` — an
`EventStream` *subclass*, because `as_event_stream` passes an `EventStream`
through untouched and that is what keeps `stream is events` true inside
`Refinement.fit`, so a pattern's fit cannot close the stream the series owns —
stamps `series_index`, `series_label`, `series_n`, `series_pass` and (on a reseed

comparable frame by frame; `series_pass` is what tells them apart.

**Cancelling a series returns what completed**, and that is WP-1006's rule one
rank up rather than an exception to it. A series is N *separate* refinements: the
pattern in flight is abandoned by `Refinement.fit` itself (no node, no commit,
models restored), while the patterns already walked are finished fits with
committed nodes in their own trees. Raising would throw those away. What must not
happen is silence, since a short `entries` list is indistinguishable from a
shorter series that ran to completion — hence `SEQUENTIAL_CANCELLED`, which names
how many of how many were reached. A cancelled forward chain also skips the

supply is the protocol: mode, plan, 2θ limits and excluded regions all come from

disagree with the single-pattern fit beside it about what is being fitted. The run
rides the one run machine as `kind: "series"`, and "pattern k of N" reaches the run

**[1035](../wp/1035-symmetry-surfaced.md) closed 2026-08-05: a phase's space group
stopped being one read-only string quoted in three places and explained in none —
and the feature turned out to be sitting on top of a bug that had nothing to do
with symmetry.**

The user's question was *"where is the space group / symmetry information?"* and
the honest answer was that everything the symmetry *does* — a tied `b`, a locked
angle, a site with two coordinate DOFs instead of three, an ADP basis of four
patterns — showed in the parameter table as an effect with no named cause. The
answer is two tiers, and **the split is a measurement rather than a taste**: the
phase facts are one `get_spacegroup` call, so they ride on `GET /api/structure`
(refetched on every head move, including one a `set_vary` made) as a `symmetry`
arm and a `causes` map; a Wyckoff letter is a spglib search per atom, and nobody
had ever timed it. It costs **1.8–8.7 ms an atom** — 13 ms for NAC's six sites,
13 ms for LaB₆'s two — so a forty-atom structure would put 0.1–0.3 s on every
head move. It went on `GET /api/structure/symmetry`, opened deliberately, which
is the escape `structure()`'s own docstring had already named. The letter earns
that twice over: it brings the **oriented site-symmetry symbol** (`.3.`, `4m.m`)
with it, which is the one form of "which symmetry element is responsible" that
names an element rather than counting a stabiliser.

`causes` is deliberately partial. `ParameterRow.held_because` says "structurally
fixed by symmetry **or by the model**", a sentence with no subject; `causes`
supplies the subject wherever the subject is symmetry and **stays silent
everywhere else**, because a locked `lor_strain` is held by the Stephens block
and claiming a symmetry element there would be worse than the anonymous version.
`held_because` itself was not touched: `ParameterRow` mirrors `Entry` field for
field with two declared extras, and a third one days before the freeze is not a
thing to add for a tooltip.

**The editing half was where the WP's own founding claim got tested, and it held
— but not where it pointed.** The draft said `PATCH /api/structure` already
accepted a changed symbol and would then 500 on the next `GET /api/params`.
Measured before any fix: it does, committing `n0003` from a snapshot that never
builds a `ParameterTable`, after which `params()` raises a bare `ValueError`. But
*nothing about that is specific to the space group* — every symmetry refusal
lives in table construction and `Refinement.edit` performs none of it — so the
gate went one level down into `_edit`, the one funnel every whole-model verb
passes. It tests the **candidate**, which is what keeps an edit that repairs a
broken head from being refused by it; the escape path is asserted, 500 and all.

The preview above it duplicates no rule: copy the structure, swap the symbol,
build a real `ParameterTable`, and read the answer off it. A table stops at the
**first** bad item, so the per-atom pass probes one atom at a time — a real table
each time, never a parser for sentences this package owns elsewhere. Five things
that diff structurally cannot see get notes instead: a setting change, a centring
change, the `_free_paths` casualties (dropped *and* renumbered — `…dof.k` is
positional, and a surviving path with a new direction behind it does not warn
anywhere today), an orbit collision, and the orbit multiplicity.

**The last of those is the session's finding, and it came from a browser.** Real
NAC, `I 21 3` → `I 41 3 2`: every stabiliser keeps its order, every site keeps
its DOF count, the ties are the same and the centring is the same, so the entry
diff is *empty* and the panel read "no parameter gains or loses a tie". What
actually happens is that every orbit doubles — 12→24, 8→16, 24→48 — the cell
holds 168 atoms instead of 84, F is computed over twice as many, and the phase
scale (and any QPA fraction off it) means something else. jsdom could not have
found it: it is not a rendering fault but a crystallographic question about which
symbols a person actually types. The same pass caught the mirror-image mistake —
a *refused* preview was being given consequences, printing "the cell would hold
198 atoms" for an `R -3 c` over a 90° cell, a number obtained by applying
operators the cell cannot carry.

One judgement worth keeping visible: an orbit collision **blocks only when the
shared occupancies sum past 1**. Two atoms on one orbit is standard modelling —
Na at 0.5 beside Ca at 0.5 — and F is right for it; what is wrong is the same
site at full occupancy twice. The criterion is physical rather than a guess about
intent, and a pre-existing collision never blocks, or a project with a mixed site
could not change its symmetry at all.

Two corrections for the record. NAC is **`I 21 3`, No. 199, six atoms over four
species** — this WP's own acceptance section and WP-1009's format sketch both say
`Ia-3d`, and `tests/data/cod_1000236.cif` says otherwise. And the `sites` arm's
docstring had called the Wyckoff letter "expensive" since WP-1014 with no number
behind it; it is 1.8–8.7 ms, which is what makes the refusal correct rather than
merely cautious.

**[1034](../wp/1034-panel-layout.md) closed 2026-08-05: the model editor and the
text document became tabs beside the plot — a decision the user had taken
without numbers, and the numbers were fetched before a line was touched.**

The WP existed to revisit two decisions that had been right when taken. WP-1013
made the `.rxt` pane a mode over the whole window because its content is
line-oriented and the format's columns are aligned *so that a rectangular
selection can hit one field*, which a 340–560 px sidebar undoes; WP-1014 made
the model pane a mode because an atom table is eight columns wide. Measured on
a real NAC project in Chrome for Testing, at 1500 / 1200 / 1000 / 860 px: the
atom table's `min-content` is **448 px** (472 with the column's padding), and
the `.rxt` document has **two** widths — its editable columns end at **546 px**
with the gutter, its trailing `#` comments at **756**. Against a sidebar that
clamps at 340–560 and drags to 72 % of the window, that makes WP-1013's sentence
true at the floor and **false at the ceiling**: at 560 px not one of 165 lines
loses an editable column, while 110 lose a comment. So the decision stood and
acquired a condition, and the condition is the rest of the WP — the reflow, the
wrap, and a full-window layout that is now *the column expanding, tab strip and
all*, one hatch for eight panels rather than a private mode for two.

The browser pass found four things, and the sharpest one is a rule: **a defect
can be invisible until two panels share a screen.** An edit discards the fitted
curves server-side — `refine.set_values`, "the fitted curve and statistics
described the *old* values" — but the run *frame* survives it, so the header
printed `Rwp 9.582%` beside a plot saying "No fitted curves yet". Nothing about
that is new; it simply could not be seen while the editor covered the plot. The
other three were layout arithmetic that jsdom cannot hold an opinion about: three
columns sharing free space equally gave the structure column 306 px of a 1000 px
pane while the two that needed less had plenty (each column now *starts* at its
own floor); a lone wrapped tab stretched into a banner; and the header pushed
`Cancel` and `⌘K` 118 px past the window's edge at 860 px, because the row's only
shrinkable item is the filename and it had already collapsed to nothing.

What the WP does **not** claim is that an edit and its consequence are one
glance. Applying an edit empties the plot until the next run, by a rule one rank
down that this package applies everywhere: curves that describe values the model
no longer holds are not drawn. The glance the tabs buy is *edit → Run → the new
curves land beside the editor*, with no mode switch in the middle.

**[1033](../wp/1033-plot-range-regions.md) closed 2026-08-05: the fit range and
the excluded regions became visible and selectable — and the plan's own premise,
"the feature is done; only the pixels are missing", was half wrong.**

The premise held for the model, the mask, the verb, the persistence and the text
surface, all of which had landed in WP-1005 and WP-1009 and needed nothing. What
it missed is that **a mask is invisible in a picture of its own output.**
`compile_model` masks before a `RefinementResult` exists, so every payload the
frontend can fetch carries only the surviving channels: measured on the
synthetic fixture, a 3–24° pattern comes back as 8.005–18.990° once limits are
set, with zero points inside a 3° exclusion. Shading alone would therefore have
drawn a band over a hole, and the fit range would have had no *outside* at all,
because the axis autoranges inside it. Half the WP is server work the plan did
not contain — an `excluded` arm on `/api/result/window` and on the raw peaks
pattern, and `Project.fitted_mask()` as the one authority for which channels the
next run fits, pinned to `compile_model` by asserting `len(result.two_theta)`
against the mask's sum.

The same measurement produced the WP's most useful piece of UI. **Settings
persist on the verb and curves move only on a run**, so between an exclusion and
the next fit the picture actively contradicts the setting — 586 measured points
inside a new band, still in the residual on screen. The route now says so
(`stale`, compared on the fitted 2θ *values*, since two masks can keep the same
count), and the strip prints the channel count beside it, which is the WP's own
acceptance criterion made continuously visible: a band drawn over points still
in the residual is worse than no band at all.

Two rules survive it. **A pointer meaning that is ambiguous everywhere is a
mode, not an arbitration**: WP-1027 could resolve the peak-drag overlap by
making the grab radius readable, because the ambiguity was local, but a region
drag is a zoom drag at every distance — so arming is explicit, hands the drag to
plotly's own select box, suspends the peak verbs, and disarms after one
selection. And **protocol must not wear a drawing choice's clothes**: the
residual selector and the intensity scale are session-local and unpersisted,
while a region changes Rwp, so the two live in different registers on one plot.

The browser pass earned its keep for the fourth session running, with two
defects jsdom structurally cannot see: **a shape bound to a data axis takes part
in the autorange** — bands drawn past the data to survive a zoom-out *became*
the range, −40 to 100 on a 0.5–59.99° pattern — and `paintRaw`, which builds its
payload by hand, silently had no masked points, in the one view a project has
before its first fit. `viz/` deliberately does not shade: a result cannot say
what was excluded, so an exported figure shows the exclusion as absence, and
teaching three renderers otherwise would be the WP-1029 (s) bug shape rather
than its cure.

**[1039](../wp/1039-search-line-count.md) closed 2026-08-05: the question was
"how many lines", the answer was "which", and the published rule that motivated
the WP turned out not to hold for this package at all.**

`DEFAULT_SEARCH_LINES` took the first twenty lines in 2θ order, which assumes the
lowest-angle components are the phase's strongest reflections. That is true of a
lab pattern opening after its first Bragg peak and false of anything opening on
background: on 11-BM NAC, which starts at 0.76° 2θ, the first twenty hold **six**
of the true cell's lines against 268 of the whole 285, the other fourteen being
components at 0.1–0.6 % of the strongest line's intensity. Ranking by intensity —
ties broken by Q, so a position-only list is untouched — gives 20 of 20 at the same
count, and `engines.search_line_order` is now the one seam both engines select
through.

**Raising the count instead was measured and is worse than expensive: it loses
answers.** Oishi-Tomiyasu (2014) argues that a false line costs only computation
while a missing one costs success, and Conograph drives on up to 48 lines on that
basis. The argument rests on its enumeration succeeding by a *membership* condition
on the observed q set, which adding elements cannot break. Ours is not a membership
condition — `indexes_the_search_lines` demands all but `n_unindexed` of the driven
set, an **absolute** budget — so every foreign line admitted spends it and past
that the true cell is *refused*, not out-ranked. Zircon carries 16 foreign lines
among 68 and loses its certified lattice entirely going from 20 to 32. The two
knobs WP-1026 called different are one knob.

Three of the WP's own premises did not survive reading the source rather than the
summary of it, which is the WP-0501 b₂ precedent paying out again: the asymmetry is
**asserted** in a single prose sentence about a single added element, not proved;
48 is an **upper threshold** on an AUTO value, not a default; and the celebrated
18–250× zone-sorting speedup was **never timed** — it is 1/rate² computed from
measured selection rates under the very cost model this package's own rule says is
not a profile. The paper also uses no intensities anywhere and never declines to,
so ranking by them contradicts nothing published.

Two dividends beyond the fix. **SRM 660c stopped reporting `engines_disagree`**:
`trial_error` had not been failing to find the certified cell, it had been solving
from the wrong base lines, because its pool took the lowest-Q lines of the whole
list. Drawn from the selection, both engines find it, and the acceptance row that
asserted the disagreement now asserts its absence — though a four-system search
still stops at `low` on `fom_panel_disagrees`, so agreement was necessary and not
sufficient. And **WP-1026's "truncating NAC's 2θ was measured and does not work —
do not spend that hour again" is withdrawn**: it was measured with the broken
selection, and redone at ≤ 32° the truth now ranks first, cubic I, −22 ppm, in
10.5 s, against a recorded −5967/+8189/+7997 ppm at cubic P and 300–620 s. NAC at
full range is still blocked, and that obstruction is now *demonstrated* rather than
assumed to be the reflection ceiling.

**The rank is not free, and half its price was avoidable.** It took the indexing
acceptance file from **6:10 to 25:19** with every row still green — cost, not
correctness — and profiling rather than reasoning found it in two fixtures: the
corundum searches ran 662 s and 596 s against the ~45–50 s recorded when they were
written. The mechanism is one line: dichotomy sizes the recursion's trial set by
the largest Q among the *driven* lines, so letting the rank reach a lab pattern's
high-angle tail (corundum's twenty span 25.5–150.1° where the low-Q rule gives
5.2–76.8°) enlarges that set for every box. Drawing the strongest N from the lowest
2N instead brings it to **11:58**, 36/36 either way, at a few lines of selection
quality (NAC 20/20 → 18/20) — and it is safe where it matters because a list of 2N
lines or fewer is selected exactly as the unbounded rule would, which is why SRM
660c's new engine agreement survives it. A calibration worth keeping from the same
measurement: two settings producing *identical* selections timed 72 s and 93 s, so
1.29× on byte-identical work is the noise floor any factor has to beat.

Nothing else moved: 35 of the 36 indexing acceptance rows are unchanged, and the
whole bethanechol benchmark selects exactly as before because bare position lists
carry no measured intensities. A process note worth keeping: the one
`validation_matrix.py` Claim this touched had prose **two WPs** out of date and
nothing failed, because the per-Claim meta-tests check structure, not what the
string says.

**[1038](../wp/1038-shift-reflection-pairs.md) closed 2026-08-04: a systematic
2θ shift is measurable before there is a cell, and the two source papers'
recipes for doing it both failed on real data.** The physics is exact and older
than the package: two reflections whose planes are integer multiples satisfy
`m·sin θ_B = sin θ'_B` for *any* lattice, so each such pair is one equation in
the shift and none in the cell (Dong, Wu & Chen 1999). `indexing/pairs.py`
recovers SRM 660c's displacement as **+0.0345°** with no reference at all —
within 0.4σ of the +0.0367 ± 0.0015° the reference-based screen fits and 0.83 of
the +0.0415° its recorded geometry predicts — and corundum's as **−0.0639°**
against an independently measured −0.065°. It declines on all ten bethanechol
sets and on HL2, which is Le Bail (2004) §VII's published failure reproduced
from our own data rather than quoted. `quality.py`'s founding thesis, that a
shift is identifiable only against a reference, was **half wrong and the useful
half**: the *cause* needs one, the *magnitude* does not.

Two published prescriptions did not survive the corpus, and neither failure was
visible from the fast tests. **DICVOL04's sign-category rule is not a test at
scale**: it admits the 11-BM NAC list on a margin of 84 out of 1838 pairs — pure
chance at that count — and reports −0.09° for a pattern whose shift is zero,
while admitting SRM 660c, whose answer is right, on a binomial z of 1.15. It was
written for the ~10 pairs a 12-line 00l list offers. Concentration against a
seeded structureless null replaces it and separates every fitted list (z 4.2–16.6)
from every bare position list (z ≈ 0), at a *measured* false-positive rate of 1 in
3600 replicates.

**And the obvious allowance broke five acceptance rows with 156 fast tests
green.** Sweeping the ranking against the window rather than reasoning about it —
the fourth session running where that was the lesson — showed corundum keeps its
certified trigonal *R* lattice through σ_sys = 0.070 and flips to a wrong
hexagonal *P* at 0.0767, and that SRM 660c at 0.060 returns a 35.9 Å³ cell
**293 000 ppm** from its certificate *at `high` confidence*. Window width alone
manufactured the confident wrong singleton the entire indexing block is built to
prevent, which makes a matching tolerance a **correctness** parameter and the
sweep the artefact worth reusing. The headroom now scales the amplitude's standard
error, never the pair-to-pair scatter, which overshot corundum's own breaking
point.

What it bought, and what it did not. `shift_allowance_assumed` clears on both
certified sets; corundum indexes 51 of 55 lines where it indexed 49, crossing the
0.9 bar with nothing declared, so the measured **magnitude** and the declared
**shape** are now cleanly separated — the window finds lines, the template moves
the cell (+122 → −93 ppm). But the WP was written calling this "the single
structural reason every real dataset abstains", and that was wrong: it is one of
several, `engines_disagree` / `predicted_but_absent` / `fom_panel_disagrees`
stand untouched, and `high` remains unreachable on this corpus. The remaining
causes are 1026's and 1041's.

  five** refuses **89.9 %**. The WP's item 1 was the right diagnosis — the
  volume prune really is inert, and its quoted numbers reproduce to three
  figures — attached to the wrong cause (staging, rather than interval
  arithmetic decoupling A·B·C from the off-diagonals it is constrained by) and
  the wrong phase. A plausible cost model is not a profile.

**But the mechanism that makes them fatal is about the allowance, not the peaks,

is added *in quadrature to every σ* — flat — which takes that 100× contrast to
**1.005**. Its fitted shift is +0.009 ± 0.016°, consistent with none. **An
assumed allowance is not free even when it is generous enough**: it buys a
matching window at the cost of the weighting the peak fitter measured.

**Supply what is missing and the gate reaches `high`: a = 4.156772 Å, −2 ppm,
M₂₀ 1113, zero caveats, `best_or_none()` returning a cell.** It is an
attribution probe rather than a protocol — the off-lattice components are
identified *using* the certificate — and its value is exactly that: the
pipeline's arithmetic is sound to the part per million, and **what stands between
it and a blind certified answer is a peak list**.

**Two gaps fell out, both filed to [1028](../wp/1028-robustness-external-data.md).**
`SearchSpec.sigma_sys_deg` and `ShiftScreen.sigma_sys_deg` are 4.3× apart and
share a name: the screen reports the scatter a template *leaves* (0.0078°), the
search needs the window the *uncorrected* positions span (0.037°), since
`refine_with_shift` runs only after a candidate survives — so declaring the
measured one silently finds nothing. And **a geometrical ambiguity the
enumeration cannot see from one side**: tetragonal P at (a/√2, a) is *exactly*

enumerates *sublattices* of index 2-4 — supercells — so a half-volume rival is
not in the enumeration: 0 partners from the cubic side, while from the tetragonal
side the cubic is found with **zero** discriminating reflections. The gate
refuses `high` to a candidate with a partner, so whichever of an isospectral pair
is the larger cell can be promoted while its equal cannot.

**A certified pattern now indexes to its certificate, and the thing that had been
costing it 2800 ppm was a performance filter (2026-07-30, third session).**
[1026](../wp/1026-indexing-acceptance.md) is still in flight. The corundum acceptance
row asserted that an uncalibrated lab pattern costs ~2800 ppm on c; it does not,
and neither of the two explanations the tree had offered for it — the matching
tolerance, then the `refine_with_shift` accept rule — was the cause.

**`_box_key` was.** Dichotomy hashes a converged box so one cell is not refined once
per sibling leaf, and it divided A..F by `max|af|` — a 0.1 % grid on the largest
component is a **~1 %** grid on the smallest, and for a long axis C = 1/c\*² *is* the
smallest. The whole trigonal-R domain on this pattern converges to **eleven leaves**;
three hashed onto a sibling and were **skipped before being refined**, and one of the
three held the certificate's c. Binned per component now, and the row asserts the
protocol a user with a standard would run: nothing declared gives trigonal R ranked
first at a **+101 ppm**, c **+16 ppm**; declaring `shift_template="cos_theta"` gives
**−73 / −126 ppm** with a fitted **−0.0606 ± 0.0138°** shift against the −0.065°
displacement [1023](../wp/1023-engine-montecarlo.md) measured independently. **A
performance filter's failure mode is a wrong answer, not a slow one** — and a skipped
leaf leaves no trace at all: no candidate, no diagnostic, nothing in `stats`, which
is why three earlier probes came back clean before a leaf-by-leaf trace found it.

**Two more defects fell out of chasing it.** The FoM panel *ranked* candidates in a
window the search never used — the fitted σ (0.0045°) rather than σ ⊕ the allowance
(0.0502°) — so `indexed_fraction` read 0.11-0.20 on candidates the search itself had
indexed at 0.65-0.89, every candidate was refuted by `indexed_fraction_low` whatever
its merit, and the Borda order was decided among cells that had all matched almost
nothing. `fom_panel` now takes the matching window separately from the measurement:
coverage members widen, M₂₀ and F_N keep the measured σ for their discrepancy floor.
And a candidate carrying a shift is scored on the **corrected** positions it claims
(`engines.scored_positions`) — without that, applying a declared template everywhere
dropped the certified lattice out of the top six, because the panel was effectively
ranking on how little a candidate had been corrected.

**The accept rule was decided, and the honest framing is that it changed nothing
here.** χ²_red is the wrong gatekeeper for a declared template — the column always
costs a degree of freedom while a cell that has already absorbed the shift cannot gain
enough χ² to pay for it — so only identifiability refuses one now. But the previous
session's claim that this was *losing the accurate cell* was itself an artifact of the
hash: with `_box_key` fixed, both rules return the same answer on corundum.

**And a refuting caveat fires on correct cells.** `predicted_but_absent` counts 11-12
reflections the *lattice* R-3m allows and the R-3c c-glide forbids, because the lattice
group is the only model that exists before `determine_extinction_symbol` runs. So any
phase with space-group extinctions refutes its own correct cell, and `high` is
unreachable for a second structural reason. It is the blind spot
`predicted_seen_fraction` already documents, promoted into a caveat that refutes;
filed to [1028](../wp/1028-robustness-external-data.md), not fixed, because the fix is
running [1025](../wp/1025-extinction-symbol.md)'s screen inside the gate.

**Seven source papers were read against the tree and three of its claims did not

the deliverable is the audit, and it is filed in
[1026](../wp/1026-indexing-acceptance.md)'s handover and the new

**Fixed — the fast suite was red and nobody had seen it.**
`test_niggli_reduction_is_unimodular_invariant` was failing on a hypothesis
example earlier runs had not generated, and `.hypothesis/` is gitignored so CI
would not have replayed it either: the tree was green by luck. The reduced cell
of (3, 3, 3, 66°, 110°, 65°) has **b = c exactly**, so Křivý-Gruber step A2 must
break the tie on |η| ≤ |ζ|, and at gemmi's default the two settings came back
with β and γ **swapped** — while gemmi's own `is_niggli` called both True.
gemmi's ε is an *absolute* 1e-9; Grosse-Kunstleve *et al.* (2004) prescribe a
*relative* ε = 1e-5·V^(1/3) in the reduction **and** in the predicate, and report
their Test 3 — which *is* our test — failing at 1e-10 and passing at 1e-5. Since
`same_lattice` dedups on the reduced A..F, the failure mode was one lattice
counted as two and the gate denied its agreement. **1353 passed / 66 skipped.**

**Superseded the same day — `refine_with_shift` refuses its own correction on
exactly the candidates that need it.** The reasoning held and the rule was changed
(see the session above); the *diagnosis of the corundum row* did not. Traced on
corundum, the template reached both engines, was called 17 times in dichotomy and
**declined 9**, every decline on the χ²_red comparison, with the ranked-first
candidate among them keeping c +2799 ppm out. That candidate turned out to be a

**Not fixed, and the one that can lose a right answer: `volume_envelope` is a
mean line, not an envelope.** Smith (1977) turns out to be **triclinic-only** and
to publish **no per-system factors at all**, so WP-1019's open item closes as
*there is nothing to check the derived scalings against* rather than *checked and
agree*. Our two constants are exactly the paper's. What is wrong is the status:
Smith quotes an average discrepancy of 10.6 % with deviations **−29 % to +32 %**,
the low side being the ordinary case since it is what missing weak lines produce
— and we call it a "bound" and feed it to the engines as a hard ceiling. With p
the detected fraction of possible lines the ratio to truth is 1.4025·p, so it
**excludes the true cell below p = 0.713**, which is that same −29 %: no margin
against the worst pattern in the paper's own calibration set. Worse,
`VOLUME_ENVELOPE_SLACK = 1.5` exists but guards only the *flagging* path while
the fatal one takes the raw envelope, and the test that validates it feeds a
*complete* line list (p = 1.0), the most favourable regime there is. Docstrings
and the manual now say "estimate" with the numbers; the behaviour is
[1030](../wp/1030-engine-scaling-low-symmetry.md)'s and
[1028](../wp/1028-robustness-external-data.md)'s.

**And the monoclinic no-go now has a measured cause, which is not what either
hypothesis said.** On the published bethanechol cell's *own twenty exact lines*,
V ∈ [800, 1200] Å³, `n_unindexed = 0`: **0 candidates, budget expiry at 300 s,
5.74 M boxes** (12 Å) and 5.82 M (16 Å) at ~15 rows/box. So it is not the
tolerance, not the peak list, and **not** a `MAX_GRID_CELLS` frontier overflow —
the cost is the number of boxes generated and tested, ~52 µs each. The cause:
`_det_interval` over the domain is [−4.80e-5, 6.40e-5], **det_lo negative**, so
the "cell too small" half of the volume test can never fire, and it is still
negative one grid cell down — a declared V ∈ [800, 1200] window prunes
essentially **nothing** until the last of four dimensions is cut. The first
hypothesis (that the A..F reparameterisation loses the prune to interval
inflation) was **refuted**: 1.1–3.2× at grid-cell scale, 93 % of the prune
survives. The real difference is structural — DICVOL's `V = A·B·C/sin β` is a
monotone separable product, so the volume shell is a **loop bound**; ours is a
**test**. `det G* = B·(A·C − E²/4)` is monotone in C at fixed A, B, E, so cutting
E before C restores it. With Louër & Louër (1972) Table 1 now in hand (the
data-derived parameter floors, transcribed into 1030 — and its non-collinearity
footnote on d₁/d₂ is exactly what a guess would have got wrong), plus the
redundant per-centring pass, the ranked items are worth ~6.5× on monoclinic.

**On the third engine: ITO is buildable and is *not* a fix for the shift
problem.** Visser (1969) reduces the continuously-searched dimension to **one at
every stage in every system** — monoclinic becomes three sequential 1-D
coincidence searches instead of a 4-D box, at ~4×10⁴ scalar operations. But the
hoped-for zero-point immunity is refuted by its own algebra: under Q → Q + δ the
four (m,n) branches of `R` shift by −1.00, −2.00 and −1.75 δ, so a constant
offset **splits** the coincidence peak rather than translating it — worse than
shifting, since the acceptance test is a count — and errors amplify 3×. It also
carries no exhaustiveness claim, so it can never contribute to `search_complete`.
TREOR's short-axis test is the same dimensionality reduction for a fraction of
the work, inside the engine we already have. **Do the cost items first and
re-measure**; if monoclinic becomes affordable, a third engine buys confidence
rather than capability, which is a weaker argument for it.

**Finally, one grading correction.** 1026's criterion 1 ("global score ≥ +9") is
mis-set: `first_4` is an **oracle over four programs**, and no entry in Table 5
reaches +9 — the individual globals are ITO13 −14, DICVOL91 −8, TREOR90 −4,
McMaille +5, Crysfire 2003 +6, and Crysfire is itself a suite. Restated in the WP

**A certified pattern indexes, and the thing that had stopped it was our own peak
list (2026-07-30).** [1026](../wp/1026-indexing-acceptance.md) is in flight and its
first result inverts [1023](../wp/1023-engine-montecarlo.md)'s diagnosis. Corundum's

**The gate could not have refused them, and the reason is a limit of ΔBIC rather
than a bad threshold in it.** ΔBIC asks whether the data prefer n+1 components to
n. That is the same question as "is there a line here" *only while the
n-component model is capable of fitting*. On the corundum 104 line χ²_red is 17.4
at n = 1 and 4.6 at n = 2 — both refuted — so any extra component wins by a mile.
Hence the third condition of `_not_separable`: the group's own fit must still be
refuted, at 3σ of χ²_red's *own* scatter rather than a flat bar, since groups
differ 5× in size across one pattern. The component stays in the model (removing
it displaces the real line by 0.010°) and is barred only from `usable()`. Three
mechanisms were excluded by measurement first: axial asymmetry (declaring
apertures gives 63 → 56 and takes χ²_red 2.9 → 10.7), the width bounds, the
background envelope. General, not a corundum quirk: satellites were **4-21 %** of
picked lines on all eight bundled real datasets, now 0-7 %.

**A second latent defect fell out of the same session, and it is the sharper one:
a precision nobody measured was refusing to index.** `assess_peak_list` abstained
on median σ(Q)/Q without asking where σ came from, so all **ten** sets of the
published bethanechol benchmark were refused — including the synchrotron set whose
published M(20) is **197** — on the strength of `PEAK_ASSUMED_ESD_DEG`, a number
this package chose. That is the module's founding rule inverted: an assumed
precision must never be quoted as a measured one, and refusing on it is quoting
it. The test now runs only on `source == "fitted"`; the figure is still computed
and reported, it simply has no vote.

**And the 2004 paper's own open question now has an answer, which is "you cannot
tell".** It hypothesised the two entries' large zeroshift was a specimen
displacement and had no way to check, every program of the day fitting one
constant zeropoint. Asked properly with three nested single-template fits, the
answer is `separable = False` on all ten sets at collinearity **1.0000** — over
6-31° 2θ, cos θ ≈ 1 and sin 2θ ≈ 2θ. The *magnitude* is determined and disagrees
with the paper: 43-1748 carries +0.062° and 46-1964 +0.058°, not the quoted 0.10°,
so subtracting 0.100 overshoots — which is why Table 5 does not show C as
uniformly easier than A.

**The workflow now reaches the space group — as a *class*, which is what a powder
measures (2026-07-30).** [1025](../wp/1025-extinction-symbol.md) landed:
`determine_extinction_symbol(data, candidate, instrument)` enumerates every gemmi
setting whose **lattice** is the indexed one, groups them by identical absence sets
over the hkl in range, fits each class by Le Bail with the profile frozen at one
shared pre-fit, and scores them by ΔBIC against the absence-free lattice. Measured
on fluorapatite, whose group the GSAS-II tutorial refines as P 6₃/m, the answer is
`P 63 - -` listing {P 6₃, P 6₃/m, P 6₃22} — the truth is *inside* the answer and is
not the answer, because those three produce identical patterns by construction. On
NAC the answer is the absence-free I class, which is also correct and for a sharper
reason: I 2₁3's screw axes are invisible **in principle**, since I-centring already
extinguishes the very reflections the 2₁ would.

**Its method result is that a detector's null model has to contain the competing
hypothesis's own predictions.** The plan said to reuse WP-1024's
`absent_reflections` against the fitted background; measured, that refutes the true
class. FAP's forbidden 003 sits **0.89 FWHM** from an allowed neighbour ten times
stronger, whose tail fills the ±½ FWHM window to **+27.6 σ**. Against the class's
own `y_calc` — background plus every reflection the class still allows — the same
window reads **−3.9 σ**. The fix keeps one detector: the same function, called with
`y_calc` in the `y_background` slot, which reduces to WP-1024's test exactly where
nothing is predicted nearby. WP-1024's version was right for what it did, because a
phantom reflection sits in a gap. Two counts carry the rest of the epistemics:
`n_added` counts only the **testable** absences (in range, and separable from every
line the class still allows), without which a class whose absences all hide under
neighbours wins on parsimony alone; and the absence of a *line* is asked of the
whole orbit, since `P a -3` extinguishes 012 but not 021 and they share one 2θ.

**Indexing works end to end (2026-07-30).**
[1024](../wp/1024-indexing-consensus.md) landed, so `index_pattern` is now a peer of
`refine()`: a raw pattern goes in, peaks are picked, both engines search, their
candidates are merged as reduced cells, ranked on the whole figure-of-merit panel,
screened for geometrical ambiguity, validated against the **whole profile** by a Le
Bail fit, and gated on **agreement**. Measured on a synthetic LaB₆ pattern picking
its own peaks, `best_or_none()` returns a = 4.15659 Å against a true 4.1566 — 2 ppm
— at `high` confidence, while the cubic F and I supercells that index *every*
observed line and tie the truth on every forward-looking figure come back `low`,
refuted by `predicted_but_absent`. `rietx index <file> --wavelength λ` does the
same from a shell and carries the verdict in its exit status; `task="index"` is the
agent surface's fourth branch, answering in its own arm because its answer has no
cell in it.

**The gate's ceiling is a result, not a shortfall — and on real lab data it is
currently unreachable, deliberately.** `high` means every engine that ran found the
same lattice and *nothing* qualifies it; two engines is the ceiling because
[1023](../wp/1023-engine-montecarlo.md) is a measured no-go. But both engines widen
their matching window by an **assumed** systematic allowance whenever no shift has
been measured — which is the normal state at index time — and a cell found inside a
widened window absorbs the shift (+1400 ppm measured). That raises
`shift_allowance_assumed`, which caps confidence on its own. So a real pattern with
no internal standard tops out at `medium`, and the way to clear it is *evidence* — a
calibrated `sigma_sys_deg`, or reference positions — never a bigger constant. Same
posture as Layer 1's abstention, one rank up; 1026 owns closing it.

**Two of the three engine rows' plans were contradicted by measurement, and so was
1024's.** Its context section named Layer 0's `unmatched_calc` as the
predicted-but-absent detector. It cannot work, structurally: Le Bail extraction
assigns each reflection `max(y_obs − y_bkg, 0)`, so a reflection predicted where
there is nothing gets nothing and **produces no negative residual at all** — what
the detector finds instead is 5σ noise excursions near a tick. Measured, it fired on
**17 of a certified cell's own 28 reflections** and 94 of a doubled cell's 153, 61 %
either way. Asking the question directly against the *fitted* background separates
them cleanly (0/28 against 117/153). The general form is worth carrying: **a detector
built for a Rietveld residual does not transfer to a Le Bail one**, because Le Bail
cannot over-predict — it fits whatever is there.

**And the three detectors that do work catch different failures, which is why none
of them is the score.** Same four cells, same pattern: Rwp is decisive on a wrong
*metric* (0.98 against a correct 0.22) and nearly silent on an *oversized* one
(0.379 — a gap smaller than the spread between specimens), where only
`predicted_but_absent` sees it; the wrong metric is caught from the other side by
`unmatched_observed` (95 observed peaks with nothing calculated). This is v0.5's
method result in a new costume: **judge a step by what it makes visible, not by
Δ Rwp.**

**Two more WP-1020 defects surfaced, and both were invisible until a consumer needed

with a partner, the indexer could never have answered at all. And
`bravais_screen` handed a Niggli-reduced cell's *input* centring back to
`find_lattice_symmetry`, but the reduction already consumed it: gemmi called a cubic
I lattice **trigonal** (6 lattice rotations instead of 24) while spglib correctly
said `Im-3m`, so the two "disagreed" on every centred candidate and capped half of
all real structures at `medium` for good. The ambiguity defect had a *passing test
asserting the wrong behaviour*, with reasoning that read only the observed positions
and ignored the absences — `indexed_fraction`'s measured blind spot, one module
over, written into a test.

**The milestone's method result so far is that every one of the engine rows was
decided by a measurement that contradicted its own plan.** 1021's plan had volume
shells and a bisection from one domain; measured, shells cost eight grid passes with
the answer's shell last, and a single depth-first stack explored 11.9 M boxes without
once visiting the cell holding the answer — the structure that works is a complete
breadth-first grid pass, then dichotomy inside the survivors. 1022's plan promised
"seconds where 1021 takes minutes"; measured, dichotomy is *faster* on every system,
and what the two engines actually buy is different **failure modes**, which is all
the confidence gate needs. 1023's plan assumed the open question was `compile_model`
cost; measured, tier 2 is affordable (13-15 ms/state) and *discriminating* (Rwp 1.29
for the certified cell against 7.25 for one 1 % off) while **tier 1 is the part that
fails**, and it fails in a way no tuning fixes: the true cell scores exactly zero.

**The session's largest finding is not about any engine — it is that a fitted
per-line σ is the wrong tolerance for real data, and it was found by accident.**
Chasing engine C's tier-1 zero meant running the two landed engines on the bundled
qarr corundum pattern, whose cell is certified, and **neither indexed it**. Fitted
σ(2θ) has a median of 0.0056° there, while the pattern's lines sit a median 0.060°
from the certified positions — a cos θ specimen displacement of −0.065°, an **11σ**
systematic — so at 3σ the true cell indexes *no lines at all*. The per-line σ that
WP-1018 worked so hard to measure is exactly right for *weighting* and exactly wrong
as a *matching window*, because it knows nothing about the systematic the pattern
carries; that is why every indexing program in the literature ships a global ~0.03°
tolerance. Both engines now add `DEFAULT_UNKNOWN_SHIFT_DEG` (0.05°) in quadrature
when no shift has been **measured**, report it with `INDEX_SHIFT_ALLOWANCE` (an
assumed precision must never look like a measured one), and consume
`SearchSpec.shift_template` through `refine_with_shift`, which corrects a *surviving*
candidate rather than the search. **That is not yet enough** — at 0.05° trial and
error still finds nothing and dichotomy ranks a wrong 618 Å³ cell first; at 0.08° the
cell comes back +1400 ppm with the shift absorbed — and the gap is handed to
[1026](../wp/1026-indexing-acceptance.md) with the numbers rather than closed by raising
a constant against one dataset.

**Four earlier defects in already-landed WP-1020 code were found by building on it**
(the two above are a fifth and sixth), each of which had been ranking a wrong cell
above the truth: `borda_scores` gave *tied*
candidates distinct ranks in input order (up to N−1 points of noise per tied member,
and two of five panel members saturate at 1.0, so most candidates tie);
`predicted_lines` counted symmetry orbits rather than lines, so cubic 333 and 511 —
both 27A, one 2θ — were two "possible lines" (fixing it is also 380× faster, which is
what had made ranking the bottleneck); `m20`/`f_n` used a plain mean discrepancy, so
one tolerated-unindexed line wrecked the score of the cell the search was right to
keep (13.2 against 62.5 for an a√5 supercell covering the impurity); and both engines
read "index all but `n_unindexed` of `n_search_lines`" as *anywhere in the pattern*,
which on a 75-line list kept 17 607 candidates. `match_lines` also built an
(observed × predicted) distance matrix where a binary search is exact, and
`dedup_candidates` re-reduced both cells on every pairwise comparison — together
worth 221 s → 10.5 s on one monoclinic search.

**[1014](../wp/1014-import-structure-editing.md) landed 2026-07-30 — data gets *in*,

**Its founding decision is a split, and it is the one to carry: if the parameter
table has the path, the parameter table owns it.** A cell edge, an occupancy, a

Without that line this panel would have become a second parameter table carrying
its own copy of the crystal systems. Measured in Chrome: one cell edit produced
exactly one `PATCH /api/params` and no model patch; one species edit exactly one

anything is created, and only a token crosses back — handing the browser a
filesystem path and taking it back would make every commit verb a path-traversal

sniff), and a CIF comes back with **`aniso_available` measured, not assumed** — the
file is read a second time with `aniso=True` and the answer is whether any site
actually had a tensor, so the checkbox that mirrors "reading a file must not
silently change what a plan frees" is offered only when there is something to opt
into. The instrument step sends a *decision* — a geometry and an anode name — and
the package supplies the wavelengths from its own NIST-scale table; a form that
posted a whole `Instrument` would be a second copy of the physics kept in
TypeScript, in the exact quantity a 100 ppm cell error hides in.

**One new verb, and one refusal moved.** `POST /api/structure/aniso` exists because
*both* of its directions are physics a client must not compute: on seeds

reciprocal axes), off restores Biso from U_eq. And `_as_structure` now refuses an

[1005](../wp/1005-project-container.md) is a `.rex/` **directory** —
`project.json`, the pattern file copied byte-for-byte, `history.jsonl`, `live/`,
`exports/` — behind `Project.create/open/save`. Its charter asked where an
*un-run* edit lives, and the answer was to delete the second authority rather than
choose between two: **`project.json` holds the settings, `history.jsonl` holds the
model state, and its head is the working state.** The tree therefore exists from
`create`, every `set_vary`/`set_values` commits a node, not one parameter value is
duplicated between the two files — and *saving is about settings, not durability*,
which is a sentence a GUI author needs before designing a close-confirmation
dialog that has nothing to confirm.

Its defect was in the history layer and it made the WP's own third task
impossible: **HEAD did not survive a reload**, twice over. `add()` advances HEAD
in memory but appends no ref record, so a log written by an ordinary `fit`
reloaded with `refs == {}` — `tree["head"]` raised. And `load()` applied every
node before every annotation, discarding file order, so an old `checkout`
overrode the three stages committed after it and HEAD came back **stale**
(measured: n0001 where the live tree stood at n0003). Records now replay in file
order, which is the in-memory semantics of an append-only log rather than a
re-reading of it. Two findings outlast it: **the reader *call* is part of a data
reference**, not just the path (a pdCIF with a `_meas` and a `_calc` block reads
as a different pattern, so `DataRef` records the reader and its options —
recording only the filename made an SRM-660c-shaped project *unopenable*), and
**two digests answer different questions** — sha256 of the bytes catches an edit,
the parsed-array fingerprint catches a *reader change*, and their disagreement is
diagnostic rather than corruption.

**Then it was re-sized, because nobody had priced it.** The shipped matrix
billed **21 minutes per push** and **1350 a month** for a nightly full suite —
about 1634 of a free-tier private repo's 2000 minutes gone before anyone

it just stops running: the first symptom would have been a month with no CI.
Now three cadences, each priced in its own workflow file — **per push** lint +

mostly made of); **weekly** the full suite plus 3.11/3.12/3.14 (55); **monthly**

The lesson is the one this milestone keeps re-learning in a new costume: a CI
matrix is a recurring-cost decision, and this one had been taken on coverage
grounds alone. The nightly/weekly split is not taste — the first design ran
the full suite on macOS nightly, which at a **10× billing multiplier** is ~400
charged minutes a night against a 2000/month private-repo quota, six times the
whole budget for one job. It was caught by arithmetic before it ran once, and
the fix was to give macOS only the coverage no other platform provides.

The WP's real deliverable is that **the bit-identity goldens' pinning stopped
being a caveat and became a measurement.** `tests/data/README.md` had warned
since WP-0401 that "a different BLAS/numpy build may legitimately differ"; the
matrix showed that sentence is half wrong. A *numpy* change does not move them
— 2.4.6 and 2.5.1, Pythons 3.11 through 3.14, all reproduce every state
bit-for-bit. A *platform* change does, on every state at once, and **nothing
else in the suite fails on Linux at all** (976 passed, 8 failed, all of them
the golden gate). What decided the design is the *shape* of the divergence:
1 ulp on quantities that are a single arithmetic chain (`theta`,
`lebail_intensity`, `pawley_x0`) and up to ~1100 ulp — 1.7e-13 relative — on
`y_calc`, which accumulates ~130 windows of transcendental evaluations. A
divergence that grows *with chain length* is a different libm and summation
order, not different code. So the gate is pinned to `darwin/arm64` and skips
elsewhere with that measurement in the skip reason, rather than being relaxed
to a tolerance: any tolerance wide enough to absorb a libm difference is wide
enough to absorb a real one, and the gate's entire content is "no refactor
changed a single computed number". The general rule is in
[DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md#testing--validation-policy).

Then the weekly run narrowed it once more, and this is the part worth carrying:
a **hosted** macOS/arm64 runner reporting the *same* numpy, scipy and
Accelerate as the capture machine reproduced 7 of 8 states and missed one by
**exactly one ulp** — with local runs bit-stable at 1/2/4/8 BLAS threads, so
not reduction ordering. The pin is to a *machine image*, which nothing visible
from Python can fingerprint. So `("darwin", "arm64")` is the right predicate
for *worth attempting* (7/8 at one ulp against 8/8 at ~1100) but not a promise,
and **no CI environment asserts these bits at all**: the weekly job reports the
comparison and fails only if the goldens *skip*. The gate is maintainer-machine
evidence, exactly the shape of the Apple-GPU gap — and is now recorded as one
rather than implied away by a green badge.

**A Windows probe was run after the scope was met, and it found a real bug.**
Not in the WP's scope (Linux + macOS) but cheap to answer by running it: the
fast suite went **7 failed → 982 passed / 115 skipped / 0 failed** on
`windows-latest`. Six of the seven failures were `'charmap' codec` decode
errors in `tests/` — the suite was less portable than the code it tests — but
the seventh was ours and user-facing: `write_qpa_table` handed `csv.writer`
output, which already ends `\r\n`, to `write_text`, so text mode translated
each `\n` again and **every row of an exported QPA table ended `\r\r\n`**.
Invisible on POSIX, which is why four milestones of acceptance runs never saw
it; its sibling `write_reflection_table` had the `newline=""` idiom right all
along. Every text read and write in the tree now names `encoding="utf-8"`
(the default is cp1252 on Windows, UTF-8 here), guarded by AST rather than
grep in `tests/test_portability.py` — a line search misses the multi-line call
that survived the first sweep. **Nothing runs Windows on a schedule**, so it is
a measurement, not a supported platform; the claim-or-don't decision is in
[1003](../wp/1003-api-freeze-pypi.md).

Two smaller results outlast the WP. **CI reports; it does not gate** —
branch protection returns 403 on a private free-plan repo, so nothing stops a
red push landing on `main`, and that is registered as a validation gap rather
than left implied by a green badge. And **the jax rows are compile-bound in a
way a cache cannot fix**: the `[dev,jax]` job costs ~8 min against ~3 for the

left to be measured rather than guessed.

**[1001](../wp/1001-validation-matrix.md) (validation matrix) landed 2026-07-29**
(1195 passed / 5 skipped in 7:37). [VALIDATION.md](../VALIDATION.md) is now the
per-assertion record of what this package has been shown to do: all 33
acceptance tests registered against a **closed eight-name vocabulary** of what
a bar can be referenced to, generated from `tests/validation_matrix.py`, and
held in bijection with the tree by an AST-collecting guard that costs ~1 s in
the fast suite. The WP's founding scope asked for three tolerance tiers; seven
referents are actually in use, and **the two the scope had no room for carry
the strongest evidence in the repo** — `characterisation` (a row asserting a
model is *inadmissible*, or that a disagreement has a particular shape) and
`prediction` (a parameter-free claim written down before the measurement).
Judged by agreement indices alone, v0.5's eight corrections would score as
having delivered nothing, so `ceiling` (`rwp < 0.20`, `gof < 2.0`) is named
explicitly as a **non**-tier. Three guards encode judgements rather than
bookkeeping: a dataset marked `consistency` may never carry a `certificate`
row (the 11-BM wavelength circularity, made executable), every acceptance
suite must *name* its dispersion setting, and the recorded default must match
the live schema.

Its second half was the one default v1.0 would freeze in the wrong position.
**`Source.dispersion` is now ON by default**, decided on measurement rather
than on WP-0504's recommendation: it is the only correction in the package
needing *no information the caller does not already have* (µR, a habit, a
strain model, a surface — dispersion wants species and λ, both already in the
model), it takes round-robin QPA from RMS 2.26 to 0.69 wt %, and the anchors
survive — SRM 660c's cell does not move at all and SRM 676a's
certificate-grade c/a goes +29.8 → +30.2 ppm against a 100 ppm bar (measured
here; 0504 never checked it) **while Rwp gets *worse*, 14.374 → 14.531 %**.
Against it, and kept: a wavelength inside an absorption-edge interval now
*raises*, because a selective fallback would leave some species corrected and
others not — manufacturing exactly the unequal cross-phase bias the correction
exists to remove.

The lasting result is not the flip but what absorbing it exposed. **21 tests
moved, and nine were bit-identity goldens with no opinion about dispersion at
all — they simply inherited it**, and the failure list could not distinguish
"this protocol deliberately excludes the correction" from "nobody thought
about it". Making every pinning test *declare* its physics fixed all nine
**without regenerating a single golden**, which is itself the evidence the
flip touched physics and not plumbing. That rule — a test that pins a number
declares its physics rather than inheriting a default — is the v0.2
protocol-adoption lesson one level up, and it is now in
[DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md#testing--validation-policy) and guarded. Two knock-ons
are recorded rather than tuned away: light-atom ADPs come back *less precise*
even as they come back *less biased* (rutile U11/U33 separate at 1.9σ with the
block on against 2.2σ without, because f″ raises the heavy atom's share), and
the calibrate→freeze→refine size/strain recovery degrades from 27 % low to
39 % — measuring it both ways showed that bar had always been marginal on a
degenerate direction.

**1032–1035 came from a use session** (2026-08-04, eleven observations plus one
question), the same provenance as 1029 and cut by *size* rather than by screen
region: 1032 is nine hour-sized repairs, 1034 is the one redesign, and neither

**[1018](../wp/1018-peak-picking.md) closed 2026-07-30**, and the row that had
carried the 🔄 glyph ("landed but not finished") is the only one that ever has.
What closed it is the **σ pull calibration** — the gate the whole downstream
tolerance model rests on, because every indexing tolerance is a multiple of the
σ(2θ) `pick_peaks` reports, and Rwp, χ² and eyeball overlays cannot see a σ that
is uniformly 40 % too small. Measured over ~1300 fitted lines per configuration,
from 100 fixed-seed Poisson realisations of a forward-model LaB₆ pattern:
`(2θ_fit − 2θ_true)/σ_fit` has **mean +0.032 / std 0.971** on a synchrotron
single line and **−0.083 / 0.980** on a lab Cu Kα doublet, against `|mean| <
0.15` and `std ∈ [0.85, 1.20]` written before the measurement. So the reported σ
is the right scale, and 1019/1020 may now tune against it.

The gate found **two more defects** — six in total for this WP, and *not one of
the six was visible by reading the code*. Both new ones are doublet physics. A
marginally resolved Kα2 has **no maximum**, so the alias filter cannot see it,
but it does have a curvature shoulder: it cleared the 5σ seeder, sat outside the
half-FWHM grouping gap, formed a **singleton group**, and came back as a line
with an esd — and the ΔBIC prune cannot refuse it, because a singleton is judged
against "no peak at all" and there genuinely is intensity there. That was one
spurious line per pattern and a **−21 mean σ pull** on the LaB₆ 110 reflection.
And the doublet amplitude ratio is not `weight`: each line diffracts at its own
Bragg angle, so it carries its own **Lorentz-polarisation** factor, and holding
the bare weight dragged the fitted Kα1 position down by 2e-4° (mean pull −0.26 →
−0.19).

Two results outlast the WP. **Sizing rule: 200 groups is enough for a `std` bar
and not for a `mean` bar** — at pull std 1 the standard error of the mean over
200 groups is 0.07, half the 0.15 bar, and a 200-group subsample of this very
ensemble read −0.15 where the converged value is −0.08; the test now asserts
`3·SE < bar` before asserting the bar, so an undersized ensemble fails loudly
instead of passing by luck. And **the remaining −0.08σ doublet bias is a
measurement, not a to-do**: 2e-5°, a fortieth of a channel, with four candidate
mechanisms excluded by substitution (exact background, isolated reflection,
per-line widths, model-σ weights) and the estimator shown unbiased to ±0.02 in
isolation — so what is left is in the detection-seeded window, two orders below
the systematic-error scale 1019 exists to model.

**[1019](../wp/1019-indexing-data-quality.md) closed the same day**, and its
deliverable is a *gate*: `assess_peak_list` judges a peak list fit to index or
**abstains with a reason**, and `fit_shift_model` attributes a systematic 2θ shift
to a zero-point error, a displaced specimen or a transparent one — or declines to,
which is the half that matters, since every program the 2004 benchmark paper
surveys fits one constant "zeropoint" instead.

Its founding question was one the plan had not resolved: **what is knowable from a
peak list alone.** Everything except the shift is a property of the list. The
shift is not — with no cell there is nothing to deviate from — so the screen is
*conditional* on reference positions, and with none the report says
`shift.source == "unavailable"` rather than reporting a zero shift it never
measured. What *is* computable with no data at all is the separability geometry of
the three templates over the angles sampled: a statement about the experiment
rather than the specimen, readable before a specimen is loaded.

Four measurements, and two of them overturned something:

- **"The cell stands when the cause is ambiguous" is true only with the word
  *competitive* in it.** Over 10-25° 2θ with a 0.10° cos θ displacement, all three
  templates' predicted corrections differ by 0.046° — nearly half the shift, a
  0.2 % cell error if the wrong one is applied. But the template that disagrees is
  the one the data *rejects*, and over the two that fit comparably the spread is
  0.0011°. The plan's conclusion survives; its reasoning is narrowed, and
  `prediction_spread_deg` now reports the number instead of a docstring asserting
  the claim.
- **A measured no-go: dominant zone and dominant row are not detectable from a
  census.** Neither is a summary statistic — a dominant zone is the statement that
  the low-angle lines satisfy a *two-dimensional* quadratic form, a dominant row an
  arithmetic progression k²B among the low Q values. Each is a search. The obvious
  census (Ito's most-repeated Q difference) was written, measured and removed: it
  scores dominant-zone cells at +0.9σ and +0.8σ against a permutation null while
  scoring a *general* monoclinic cell at +3.3σ, and against a uniform null a
  **cubic** list scores +15.6σ — it detects commensurability, not zones. A test
  asserts the diagnostic code's absence so it cannot creep back, and the engines
  (1021/1022) have been told they own the detection.
- **Smith's volume envelope needed two scalings, and the second was found by the
  envelope excluding the right answer**: with the Laue orbit factor alone,
  corundum's bound came out at 125 Å³ against a true 255 Å³, because R-centring
  extinguishes two thirds of hkl. Centring is part of the answer (1025's extinction
  symbol), so the default is the worst case each system allows — the one failure a
  search bound may not have is excluding the true cell — and the envelope is
  reported per system, since they span 96×.
- **`constant` and `cos θ` stay 0.96 collinear even over 10-140°**, so
  separability is decided on the residual-SS ratio against real data and never on
  the geometry alone.

One item was left open for the user rather than a session: the per-system envelope
scaling is *derived* here, not published, and a clean copy of Smith (1977) would
let the derived factors be checked against the paper's — the WP-0501 b₂
transposition being the precedent for why that check is worth asking for.

**Closed 2026-07-30, and the answer was that the question had a false premise.**
The paper arrived and is **triclinic-only**: it publishes *no* per-system factors,
so there is nothing to check the derived scalings against, and its own closing
paragraph names systematic absences as the obstacle to extending the method to
monoclinic and orthorhombic and leaves it unsolved. Our two constants (0.60,
0.0052) are exactly the paper's and reproduce its printed 13.39/17.24/21.32. What
the check *did* find is a defect nobody was looking for — the relation is a
least-squares **mean line** (−29 % to +32 % about a 10.6 % average), not the
upper envelope this package calls it, and used as a hard search ceiling it
excludes the true cell below a detection fraction of 0.713, which is that same
−29 %. See "Current focus" and [1030](../wp/1030-engine-scaling-low-symmetry.md).
The precedent held, in other words, but not in the direction it was invoked for:
asking for the paper was right, and the thing it caught was a status claim rather
than a transposed coefficient.

**[1020](../wp/1020-indexing-core.md) closed the same day** — five modules, 40
tests, an eleventh manual chapter, and **no engine**: the Q-space quadratic form
and its symmetry-allowed subspaces, weighted candidate refinement with an optional
shift column, Niggli/Delaunay reduction with two-opinion Bravais determination over
a tolerance sweep, the figure-of-merit **panel** scored in both directions, and
HNF derivative-lattice ambiguity with the reflections that would break each tie.
1021-1023 now have everything they share.  Full suite after all three WPs: 1251
passed / 70 skipped / 0 failed, including the `slow` real-data acceptance.

**Its lesson is about tests, not about crystallography: three of its four defects
passed the test that should have caught them.**

- The metric subspace was derived from the **transposed** rotations, and the
  dimension test passed. CLAUDE.md's "reciprocal-space action is Rᵀ" is about
  *hkl*; a tensor contracting with h twice is invariant under U → R·U·Rᵀ, and G\*
  is one. The transposed call returns the *direct* metric's invariants — the same
  dimension in every system, because the transposed set is a group too, so the
  WP's own acceptance criterion (1/2/2/2/3/4/6) was satisfied by the wrong
  subspace, with F = −A for hexagonal where the reciprocal metric has F = +A. What
  catches it is asserting the **true** metric lies in the span.
- A Gauss-Newton sign error that is locally correct: flipping only the θ block
  still solves for θ, and leaves the shift column with the wrong relative sign —
  s = −11.65 for an injected +0.05°.
- **M₂₀ was not invariant under a unimodular setting change, by 5 %**, because
  N_poss counts predictions up to the N-th observed line and that line *is* a
  prediction, so a strict comparison depends on fp rounding (N_poss 20 vs 19,
  M₂₀ 76.43 vs 80.45).
- And a perfect cell scored **M₂₀ = 0**: the figure divides by ⟨ΔQ⟩, which → 0 when
  a candidate fits within fp noise, so the obvious zero-guard ranked the right
  answer last. The mean is now floored at the median σ — a meaning rather than an
  epsilon, since a discrepancy below the measurement precision is not knowable, and
  per-line σ is what this package has and 1968 did not.

Two things it declined to do. **Four published figures of merit are not
implemented** — the Oishi-Tomiyasu reversed/symmetric de Wolff pair, WRIP20 and
McM₂₀ — because their formulas cannot be written from memory with correct
attribution, and guessing one while citing its paper is the WP-0501 b₂ failure in a
new costume; the panel's *argument* (coverage in both directions) is fully
implemented, which is what the measured §D result demands. And **1020 emits no
diagnostics at all**, deliberately: it has no answer to qualify, so
`INDEX_BRAVAIS_AMBIGUOUS` belongs to 1024 where a `CellCandidate` exists to carry
it.

1018's earlier value was already banked, and it is the v0.5 method result in
a new costume: **four defects, none of them visible by reading the code.** A
resolved Kα1/Kα2 doublet manufactured one spurious line per reflection (each

comes back as a real line — structural to per-group fitting, and any future

Jacobian — agreed with central differences to 2.5e-07 on every column first

**A process note that outlasts the WP.** 1018, [1004](../wp/1004-parameter-plan-api.md)

and most of `schemas/indexing.py` are committed inside `f63556c`, `e46ead2` and

will mislead you — start from `068149e`.** The rule this buys: one `git worktree`
per concurrent session, or only one session commits.

**1036 came from a GUI question and was not a GUI WP.** The cell ties assumed
b-unique monoclinic and hexagonal axes for R groups, and held a symmetry-fixed
angle at its *stored* value rather than at 90°/120°. Its task 1 was the sweep
that decided how much that mattered, and **the answer went both ways**: zero of
28 existing inputs reach a broken branch, so nothing shipped moved — but all
three are reachable from a plain CIF, because `read_small_structure` picks the R
setting from the **cell**, not from the symbol. The draft premise that a bare
`R -3 c` always resolves to `:H` was wrong, and measuring it is what caught
that. The lesson to carry: the free-parameter *count* was correct in every
broken case, so the degrees-of-freedom test guarding this had been passing on
the wrong subspace — WP-1020's transposed-rotation trap, one rank down.

Three decisions worth keeping visible, because each is a place the obvious
implementation is wrong:

- **Three engines, and the confidence is their agreement.** Both source papers
  conclude that no single indexing program wins and that running several is
  what raises the success rate — which is the device this package already uses
  for correctness elsewhere (`direction="both"` flagging
  `SEQUENTIAL_PATH_DEPENDENT`, the per-column cross-backend Jacobian matrix).
  The engines share only the Q form and the tolerance model.
- **Coverage is scored in both directions, and that is measured, not
  aesthetic.** On the guiLLeMot MnSb_34 screen, ranking on share-of-observed-
  intensity alone puts a 390-line phase first with 9 % of its own lines
  present, above the truth at 56.5 %. A cell that indexes everything and
  predicts a forest is the classic false positive and one number cannot see it.
- **A restricted search is never a verdict about the sample.** Measured on the

### 1077 closes (2026-08-18) — a manual finding that moved an answer

Moved from ROADMAP under protocol rule 5.

**Same provenance as 1076's nine: found by writing a chapter over the call.**
`using/indexing.md`'s session measured `determine_extinction_symbol` refuting
`R - c -` on the IUCr round-robin corundum pattern — NIST SRM 676a, certified
`R -3 c` — and returning `R - - -`, a class whose five members do not include
the certified group at all. Not an abstention; a wrong answer, from the workflow
AGENT_PROTOCOL §7d prescribes. 1076's findings all changed what a result *said*;
this one changed what a call *answers*, which is why 1.0.2's headline had to
stop claiming the release computes what 1.0.1 computed.

**The diagnosis needed a control, and the control is the whole of it.** Both
refuting positions sit on the low-angle flank of a strong allowed line, 2.76 and
1.49 FWHM out. Probing *sham* positions at the same offsets — places where no
reflection of any kind is predicted — the same 3σ test fires on 40–50 % of them,
up to 24.7σ, and **only below a line**: median +2.4σ on the low-angle side
against +0.1σ on the high-angle side, which is the unmodelled axial-divergence
tail and nothing about any absence. Without that control the refutation reads as
evidence; with it, the test is measuring the profile model. Freeing the FCJ
asymmetry closed the story from the other side — `profile_rwp` 0.149 → 0.139 and
the refutation still stands, so a better profile was never the repair.

**The fix is a second silence requirement, and it costs no new constant.** A
forbidden position is testable only where the class's *own* fitted pattern
leaves the window below the test's own detection threshold — same window, same
propagated σ, same `k_sigma`. Read as its consequence: a position survives only
when a **total** failure of a neighbour's tail could not by itself manufacture a
refutation. On corundum the two loud positions carry 20.0σ and 25.7σ of
predicted tail against 0.2–3.4σ for the seven that already read absent, so the
statistic separates them without being tuned to. The screen now returns
`R - c -` = {`R 3 c`, `R -3 c`} at ΔBIC −218 with five testable positions all
absent — the certified group listed beside the non-centrosymmetric partner no
counting time can separate from it.

**What the gate does not fix is worth as much as what it does.** Run over the
whole 5–150° range with the instrument's declared widths, the same specimen is
still refuted: the shared fit lands peaks a third too wide and the observed
excess at 56.929° is +26.9σ against 0.27σ of predicted tail — a gross profile
mismatch rather than a tail, and one no calibration of the absence test reaches
(inflating σ by the fit's own gof, 3.05, still leaves it at 8.8σ). So the gate
bounds what a *neighbour* can do and `ExtinctionScreen.profile_rwp` remains the
field that bounds what a wrong *profile* can, which is now measured as a pair on
one specimen rather than asserted.

## Appendix — the CLAUDE.md indexing dossier (moved from CLAUDE.md, 2026-07-31)

Between 2026-07-30 and 2026-07-31 the root CLAUDE.md accumulated the
WP-1018…1026 findings as ~250 lines of measured narrative; WP-1031 moved the
story-shaped blocks here verbatim and left distilled rules (with pointers
here) in the root. The WP files hold the per-session detail.

**`predicted_but_absent` cannot tell an oversized cell from a space-group
extinction, and on a real phase that is not a corner case.** It counts against the
*lattice* group, which is the only model available before
`determine_extinction_symbol` runs — so the certified corundum cell, correct to
~100 ppm, is refuted by 12 reflections R-3m allows and the R-3c c-glide forbids
(WP-1026, measured). It is the blind spot `predicted_seen_fraction` already states,
promoted into a refuting caveat. Read a firing as "this cell predicts lines the
pattern lacks", never as "this cell is too big"; the two are separable only by
running the extinction screen, which the gate does not do. **The control exists
and confirms it**: NIST SRM 660c LaB6 is P m -3 m, which extinguishes nothing, and
indexes with `predicted_but_absent` **0 of 30** and `predicted_seen_fraction`
**1.000** against corundum's 11-12 and 0.86. Which is also how to choose the next
acceptance dataset — **by its space group, not by its convenience**: pairing a
phase that has absences with one that has none is what turned this from an
argument into a measurement.

**`high` confidence is reachable on real data, and what it costs is a peak list.**
Same LaB6 pattern, indexed as picked: a is **−127 ppm** out and the grade is `low`.
Remove the five picked components no certified position explains and declare the
systematic as *measured* rather than assumed, and it is **a = 4.156772 Å, −2 ppm**
from the certification CIF, M₂₀ 1113, **zero caveats**, with `best_or_none()`
returning a cell for the first time. The five are **axial-divergence tails** (they
sit low below 90° 2θ and high above it, a sign reversal nothing else in a
Bragg-Brentano pattern has) plus one Kα2 residual, and they escape `not_separable`
for **three different reasons**, so no single threshold reaches them. Two general
lessons sit under that. **An assumed allowance is not free even when it is
generous enough**: `DEFAULT_UNKNOWN_SHIFT_DEG` is added *in quadrature to every
line's σ*, which is flat, so a 100× precision contrast between real lines and
phantoms becomes 1.005 — `fit_shift_model`, which weights by each line's own σ,
recovers the displacement (+0.0367 ± 0.0015° against a parameter-free +0.0415°
from the recorded −0.07877 mm at R = 217.5 mm) while the *search* on the identical
list fits +0.009 ± 0.016°. And **`sigma_sys_deg` means two different things**: the
screen reports the scatter a template *leaves* (0.0078°), the search needs the
window the *uncorrected* positions span (0.037°, since `refine_with_shift` runs
only after a candidate survives), so declaring the measured one silently returns
no candidate at all.

**The measured scoreboard, and it is the honest shape of this feature
(re-measured WP-1041, 2026-08-05; it is generated from the acceptance run by
`tests/indexing_gallery.py`, so re-measure by running the suite).** Across
**nine** known-cell datasets: **six** rank the right lattice first (SRM 660c
LaB6, SRM 676a corundum, zincite, zircon, **brucite** and **magnetite**),
**two** find it below first (11-BM NAC at rank 2 of 2, where the panel leads
with the P description of the truth's own axes; FAP at rank 4 of 12, behind
three cells 966-1396 ppm out that index fewer lines), and **one** is refused
before any engine starts (fluorite: 18 usable against `PEAK_MIN_USABLE_LINES`
= 20 — high symmetry makes a pattern easy to index right up until it makes it
too sparse to index at all). **Zero of the nine are promoted** —
`best_or_none()` is None on every real dataset in the file, including the six
that are right. Read that as *never wrong, and silent more often than right*,
and do not let a summary round it up.

**What the previous version of this paragraph got wrong is instructive, because
both defects were structural.** It read "eight known-cell datasets: five right,
one refused, two fail" while naming *nine* datasets across the buckets — NAC was
described inside the failure bucket without being counted, and FAP was counted as
putting the right lattice first by a sentence saying in the same breath that it is
second. Three buckets cannot hold this feature's commonest outcome: the truth is
*in the list* and something else leads it, which is neither a success nor a
failure. There are four now, and the count is computed rather than typed. The
other defect: **brucite and magnetite were prose.** They were measured once, before
WP-1030's prunes, WP-1039's search-line ordering, WP-1040's third engine and
WP-1041's dedup key, were never acceptance rows, and so were never re-run while
four WPs changed the answer underneath them. Both now rank the truth **first** —
brucite's c × 2 and c × 3 supercells sit below it at `predicted_seen_fraction`
0.43 and 0.32 against the truth's 0.86, near the exact 1/2 and 1/3 an exact
supercell must give. A number that is not a test row is a number nobody
re-measures. Three lessons transfer. **A literature cell is not a
specimen's cell**: brucite's round-robin specimen sits **+1750 ppm** from Zigan &
Rothbauer's, 30× the goniometer floor, so a mineral's published cell grades
lattice type and centring and nothing in ppm. **An indexed cell has no
displacement parameter**, so it absorbs what a refinement models — 127 ppm
measured on SRM 660c — which is why an indexing bar is looser than the same
dataset's refinement bar (FAP: 500 ppm against ±300). And **M₂₀ can invert the
ranking**: on FAP the cell both engines agree on indexes 178 of 185 lines and
sits *below* three cells 966-1396 ppm out that index 152, because a wrong metric
matching a subset tightly is what a plain mean of ⟨ΔQ⟩ rewards. (Those figures
read 181, 1218 ppm and 167 before the WP-1035 merge; the *claim* is unchanged and
FAP's row asserts membership and refusal rather than rank, which is why the drift
cost nothing there — WP-1041.)

**Geometrical ambiguity has a class the derivative-lattice enumeration cannot
reach.** `ambiguity_partners` walks *sublattices* of index 2-4 — supercells — so a
rival of **smaller** volume is not in the enumeration. One exists for the
commonest lattice there is: tetragonal P at (a/√2, a) is **exactly** isospectral
with cubic P *a*, because 2(h²+k²)+l² represents precisely the integers h²+k²+l²
does (both miss 4ⁿ(8m+7)). Measured: 0 partners from the cubic side, while from
the tetragonal side the cubic is found at index 2 with **zero** discriminating
reflections, and both engines find the rival on the real pattern. It matters
because the gate refuses `high` to a candidate with a partner — so whichever of an
isospectral pair happens to be the larger cell can be promoted while its equal
cannot.

`determine_extinction_symbol(data, candidate, instrument)` (`indexing/extinction.py`)
is the next step and keeps the same rule one rank down: it ranks the **extinction
classes** the lattice admits, each listing its space groups, because the powder
observable is the extinction symbol and groups sharing an absence set produce
identical patterns *by construction*. Classes are derived — every gemmi setting
whose **lattice** (not crystal system: a hexagonal metric carries the trigonal-P
groups) matches, grouped by identical absences over the hkl in range — never
transcribed from IT A Table 3.2, which cannot speak about a non-standard setting the
indexed axes may be in. Each class is fitted by Le Bail with the profile frozen at
one shared pre-fit and scored by `report.layer2`'s ΔBIC against the absence-free
lattice; **Rwp cannot be the score** (fewer absences ⇒ more reflections ⇒ never a
worse fit; measured, the true monoclinic class and its screw-free partner differ by
1e-5 in Rwp and 24 in ΔBIC). Three counts are decisions: `n_added` counts only
**testable** absences — in range and separable from every line the class still
allows — or a class whose absences all hide under neighbours wins on parsimony with
no measurement behind it; a *line*'s absence is asked of the whole orbit (`P a -3`
extinguishes 012 but not 021, one 2θ); and **the absence test's null model is the
class's own `y_calc`, not the fitted background** — the same
`workflow.absent_reflections`, called with `y_calc` in the `y_background` slot,
because a forbidden position sits inside a dense predicted pattern where a phantom
reflection sits in a gap (measured on FAP: the forbidden 003 reads +27.6 σ against
the background and −3.9 σ against the model, its allowed neighbour 0.89 FWHM away).

Two invariants inherited from fixing WP-1020 while building on it. **An ambiguity
partner must be refuted by the lines it needs and the data lack** — a superlattice
indexes every observed line by construction, so without that exclusion every
derivative lattice is reported (28 for a certified cubic cell) and the gate can never
promote anything; the test is asymmetric (the partner's *extra* predictions, never the
parent's own absent ones) and a surviving partner's discriminating reflections are
therefore *outside* the measured range. **A Niggli-reduced cell is primitive**, so
`ReducedCell.centring` is provenance about the input and must never be handed back to
anything that applies a centring — doing so made gemmi call a cubic I lattice
trigonal.

**But the tolerance was never why the certified pattern failed to index, and the
correction is the more useful lesson (WP-1026).** The obstruction was **our own peak
list**: `detect_peaks` proposed 41 groups with one seed each and `fit_group` returned
**63** components, adding a phantom ~1 FWHM below every strong peak at ~10 % of its
area with a small esd. With those flagged both engines rank the certified cell first,
at the same allowance. The gate could not have refused them, and not because
`PEAK_KEEP_COMPONENT_MIN_DELTA_BIC` is wrong: **ΔBIC asks whether the data prefer n+1
components to n, which is the same question as "is there a line here" only while the
n-component model is capable of fitting.** On the corundum 104 line χ²_red is 17.4 at
n = 1 and 4.6 at n = 2 — both refuted — so any extra component wins. Hence
`_not_separable` (`indexing/pick.py`), whose third and load-bearing condition is that
the group's own fit is still refuted, at `PEAK_REFUTED_SIGMA` × σ(χ²_red) above 1
rather than a flat bar because groups differ 5× in size across one pattern. The
component **stays in the model** (removing it displaces the real line by 0.010°) and
is barred only from `usable()`. General, not a corundum quirk: satellites were 4-21 %
of picked lines on all eight bundled real datasets, now 0-7 %. **A search that finds
nothing indicts its input before its tolerance.**

**That certified pattern was blocked twice, and the second cause is the one to
carry into any engine work: a leaf that is never refined is invisible.** With the
peak list fixed the search returned the right lattice type with **c +2799 ppm**, and
one session recorded that as what an uncalibrated lab pattern costs. It was
`_box_key`, dichotomy's duplicate-leaf hash, which divided A..F by `max|af|` — so a
0.1 % grid on the largest component was a ~1 % grid on the smallest, and for a long
axis C = 1/c\*² **is** the smallest. The whole trigonal-R domain converges to
**eleven** leaves; three hashed onto a sibling and were skipped *before* being
refined, and one of the three held the certificate's c. Per-component binning (log
for the diagonals, partner-scaled for the off-diagonals) recovers a = +101 ppm,
c = +16 ppm ranked first, and with the shift template declared −73 / −126 ppm at a
fitted −0.0606 ± 0.0138° against an independently measured −0.065° displacement.
**A performance filter's failure mode is a wrong answer, not a slow one**, and its
cost was 11 refinements against 8.

**Three measured facts from checking the source papers against the code
(2026-07-30), each of which contradicts something the tree asserted.** All three
are recorded in WP-1026's handover and WP-1030; only the first is fixed.

- **Niggli reduction is not canonical in floating point without a *relative*
  tolerance.** Křivý-Gruber decides its normalisation on exact equalities — with
  b = c the tie breaks on |η| ≤ |ζ| — and gemmi defaults to an *absolute* 1e-9,
  under which two settings of one lattice came back with β and γ **swapped**
  while gemmi's own `is_niggli` called both True. `NIGGLI_EPS_RELATIVE` = 1e-5,
  as ε = 1e-5·V^(1/3), used in the reduction **and** in the predicate
  (Grosse-Kunstleve *et al.* 2004, whose Test 3 *is*
  `test_niggli_reduction_is_unimodular_invariant`). Since `same_lattice` dedups
  on reduced A..F, a non-canonical reduction splits one lattice into two
  candidates and denies the gate its agreement.
- **`refine_with_shift` refused its own correction on exactly the candidates that
  needed it**, because the accept test was χ²_red and the extra column costs a
  degree of freedom, so a cell that has *already absorbed* the shift cannot gain
  enough χ² to pay for it. **Fixed**: a declared template is the caller's physics,
  like `mu_t`, and only *identifiability* refuses it now (no template, a numerical
  failure, or fewer assigned lines than metric parameters + 2). That change needs
  `engines.scored_positions` beside it — a candidate carrying a shift claims the
  **corrected** lines, so scoring it against the raw ones marks it down for its own
  correction, and applying the template everywhere with the panel left on raw
  positions dropped the certified corundum lattice out of the top six.
- **`volume_envelope` is a mean line, not an envelope.** Smith (1977) is
  triclinic-only, publishes **no** per-system factors (so the derived Laue/centring
  scalings here have nothing to check against), and quotes −29 % to +32 % about a
  10.6 % average. Used as a hard search ceiling it excludes the true cell below a
  detection fraction of 0.713 — which is that same −29 %. `VOLUME_ENVELOPE_SLACK`
  exists but guards only the *flagging* path, not the ceiling.

**And an assumed precision may never refuse to index.** `assess_peak_list`'s
`MAX_RELATIVE_SIGMA_Q` abstention is a statement about *measured* data, so it runs
only when `PeakList.source == "fitted"`. On a `from_positions` list every σ is
`PEAK_ASSUMED_ESD_DEG` — chosen here — and refusing on it quotes an assumed precision
as a measured one, the inverse of the rule above. Measured: all **ten** sets of the
published bethanechol benchmark failed it, including the synchrotron set whose
published M(20) is 197. The figure is still computed and reported
(`PEAK_POSITION_PRECISION`); it simply has no vote.

## Appendix — the McCusker 1999 compliance audit (2026-08-15)

The IUCr Rietveld refinement guidelines (McCusker, Von Dreele, Cox, Louër &
Scardi, 1999, *J. Appl. Cryst.* **32**, 36–50) are cited in `README.md`,
`ATTRIBUTION.md`, `AGENT_PROTOCOL.md` §2, `strategy/staged.py`'s module
docstring, the two `mccusker_*` plan names and `docs/manual/using/concepts.md`.
Until 2026-08-15 nobody here had read the paper — it is open access but returns
403 to an automated fetch (WP-1068 § Blocked). The user supplied it, and this
is the audit that followed: every clause of the paper that a *program* can act
on, checked against the code.

**Result: no correctness defect.** Every formula the paper writes down that
this package also implements matches it — eq (3) sample displacement, eq (5)
Caglioti, eq (6) the Lorentzian width law, eq (10)/(11)/(12) the agreement
indices, the µR ≤ 1 fence, the S = S_y + c_w·S_G restraint residual. What the
audit found is **one documentation defect** (fixed here) and **nine features
the guidelines ask for that this package does not have**.

### The documentation defect

`concepts.md` § "The order the presets encode" read: *"The preset order is the
one McCusker et al. set out in the IUCr Rietveld refinement guidelines. Three
of its rules carry more weight than they look like"* — and then listed three
rules that are **not in the paper**: `w` before `u,v,x,y`; intensity-scaling
corrections last; anisotropic strain freed inside the sample-broadening stage.
All three are this repository's own measured findings, and `AGENT_PROTOCOL.md`
§2 states them without attributing them to anyone. The manual acquired the
false attribution when that passage was compressed (WP-1068). Fixed: the
section now gives the paper's five actual ordering rules, cited, followed by
the three house rules named as house rules — plus the one deliberate departure
(the guidelines suggest heavier atoms' positions before lighter ones;
`mccusker_structural` frees every coordinate in one stage).

Two conventions differ from the paper's and are correct as they stand. Eq (12)
calls Rwp/Rexp "χ²"; here that ratio is `Statistics.gof` and `Statistics.chi2`
is the reduced χ² (= gof²), following Toby (2006) — `concepts.md` now says so,
because a number copied from a paper needs the convention copied with it. And
§7's convergence test, max shift/esd ≤ 0.10 in the final cycle, is satisfied a
fortiori by scipy TRF at `ftol = 1e-9` (on a several-thousand-point pattern
that is roughly three orders tighter), but the quantity itself is not reported.

### The nine gaps

Ordered by what they would cost a user, not by effort.

1. **No R_Bragg or R_F** (§11 eqs 13, 14). The structure-sensitive agreement
   indices. Every published Rietveld refinement quotes at least one; Young,
   Prince & Sparks (1982) require them. The pdCIF export has no structure-R tag
   at all. The machinery is already present: Le Bail partitioning computes
   exactly the "observed" integrated intensities eq (14) needs.
2. **No user-facing constraint mechanism** (§7, §8, §12.7 viii/ix, §12.8 v).
   The paper recommends, four separate times: constrain similar atoms to equal
   displacement parameters; apply chemical constraints to occupancies; use a
   rigid body to *reduce* the parameter count where restraints would *add*
   observations. This package has soft restraints (a target value) and no way
   for a user to tie one parameter to another. The machinery exists one rank
   down — `params.vector.AffineTie` / `ParameterTable.set_tie`, already carrying
   cell ties and Wyckoff DOFs — but `Refinement`'s surface is `set_vary` /
   `set_values`, and a tied row refuses an edit by design (WP-1004). This is the
   largest gap.
3. **No observation/parameter check** (§9). "The observation/parameter ratio
   should be at least three and preferably five", where an observation is a
   *unique reflection* and emphatically not a profile point — the paper's whole
   §9 is that the Rietveld algorithm will happily refine more parameters than
   the data support because mathematically N is the number of steps.
   `Statistics` reports `n_points` and `n_free_parameters` and no reflection
   count. The identifiability work (Gram condition, soft modes, exchange
   findings) answers a sharper question but not this one, and this one is the
   ratio a reader checks first.
4. **No interatomic distance / angle table** (§11). The paper's second "most
   important criterion for judging the quality of a Rietveld refinement" is that
   the distances and angles are chemically sensible. A `RefinementResult`
   reports *restrained* distances only, and those without esds — so §10's
   full-correlation-matrix rule for derived quantities, which QPA honours, has
   nothing to apply to in geometry.
5. **No difference Fourier maps** (§6, §12.7 ii, §12.8 ii). The paper's route to
   a missing atom, and its reason for holding the scale fixed until the model is
   complete. Arguably structure solution rather than refinement; the inputs (the
   intensity partition, phases from the partial model) are already computed.
6. **No restraint weight schedule** (§8). c_w "is set high at the beginning …
   [and] can then be reduced during the course of the refinement". `weight` is
   per-restraint and static; `Stage` has no restraint-weight field.
7. **No capillary sample-displacement correction** (§5 eq 4,
   Δ2θ = (x sin 2θ − y cos 2θ)/R). `debye_scherrer` moves peaks with
   `zero_shift` alone, and `Geometry`'s docstring states that as a design
   choice. Defensible at a synchrotron with a crystal analyser — the paper says
   displacement error is eliminated there — and a real omission for a laboratory
   Debye-Scherrer or Guinier camera.
8. **The esd method is only half-stated** (§10: "In any publication, the method
   used to calculate the e.s.d.'s should be stated"). The CIF export names the
   Bérar-Lelann inflation factor but not the base estimator, χ²_red·(JᵀJ)⁻¹.
9. **No step-size check** (§2: five to ten steps across each FWHM).
   `PatternDiagnostics` is model-free and already fits peaks — it reports peak
   density, signal-to-background, humps and Kβ/W ghosts — but not steps per
   FWHM. Undersampling is a data-collection error no refinement can repair, and
   the guidelines lead with it.

Listed for completeness and **not** recommended: the variable-divergence-slit
intensity correction (§2). The paper argues against collecting such data rather
than for correcting it, and notes most programs do not implement it; the readers
already refuse a divergence-slit *scan* by name (`io/formats/bruker_raw.py`).
