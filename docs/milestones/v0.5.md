# v0.5 — corrections & microstructure: measured acceptance record ✅

**Status: shipped 2026-07-28.** All eight v0.5 work packages (WP-0501…0508)
landed. Measured on an Apple-silicon Mac with Python 3.12, numpy 2.5.1,
scipy 1.18.0, jax 0.11.0 and torch 2.13.0 — every optional-backend row live.
Milestone tracking: [../ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md); design rationale:
[../DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md); the consumer-facing protocol these findings feed:
[../AGENT_PROTOCOL.md](../AGENT_PROTOCOL.md).

Delivered in v0.5: capillary absorption (0501), surface roughness (0502),
Stephens anisotropic strain (0503), anomalous f′/f″ (0504), sequential
warm-started series (0505), secondary extinction (0506), five more anode
wavelengths (0507), and flat-plate absorption plus the real-data capillary
acceptance (0508).

**What this milestone's criterion is.** v0.2 and v0.3 were measured against
certificates; v0.4 against itself (does every backend compute the same
derivative). v0.5 adds *corrections* — factors that multiply an intensity or
widen a peak — and the honest criterion for a correction is neither "Rwp
improved" nor "it matches a certificate". It is: **which physical quantity does
it unbias, and by how much.** Four of the eight rows below improve no fit
statistic at all, and one deliberately makes Rwp worse. That is the record.

## The headline: two of these corrections cannot improve the fit, and one is
## worse when it is right

**WP-0501 capillary absorption.** The Rouse, Cooper, York & Chakera (1970)
cylinder factor is *exactly* a constant × exp(c·sin²θ) — a Debye-Waller shape —
so applying it to a model with a free phase scale and free displacement
parameters is an exact reparameterisation. Validated at algorithm level against
a quadrature of the exact ITC eq. (6.3.3.4) integral across **both** arguments
(0 ≤ µR ≤ 1 and 0 ≤ sin²θ ≤ 1): max error 0.0035, exactly the bound the paper
claims for its own fit.

**WP-0508 supplied the dataset-level acceptance that WP-0501 could not.**
`tests/data` had no capillary pattern with a *stated bore and specimen*, the two
things µR needs. 11-BM's SRM 660a LaB₆ standard is one: the file header names
the specimen, and 11-BM's mail-in program supplies exactly one container
(Cole-Parmer #95820-06, **ID 0.0320″ = 0.81 mm** Kapton), giving µR = 0.47–0.81
across the plausible packing range. Measured over 2–60° 2θ, 116 001 points, the
same staged plan run with and without µR = 0.674:

| | no correction | µR = 0.674 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rwp | 0.0884883 | 0.0884884 | +3.2e-8 |
| a (Å) | 4.1568496 | 4.1568496 | −7.9e-12 |
| B(La) (Å²) | 0.453890 | 0.470545 | **+0.0166542** |
| B(B) (Å²) | 0.205395 | 0.222049 | **+0.0166542** |

against a predicted `equivalent_delta_biso` of **0.0166542**. Both atoms, seven
decimals, and the identity reproduces to 0.0166540 on top of an anomalous-
dispersion model — which is the check that "exact" means exact rather than
"fits this particular model".

Two things this suite deliberately does **not** claim, both recorded in
`tests/data/README.md` and asserted only as consistency:

* **The cell is circular here.** The file header reads
  `# Calibration from: /data/oct09/11bmb_3843.calib` — λ was calibrated at the
  beamline against LaB₆ itself. It lands 16 ppm from the SRM 660a certificate
  (4.1569162(97) Å at 22.5 °C; this scan at 295.0 K accounts for ≈ −4 ppm of
  that), which is worth quoting and is not an anchor. The absolute anchors
  remain SRM 660c and SRM 676a.
* **The absolute Biso is not a reference value.** Switching on anomalous
  dispersion moves B(La) by −0.044 Å², 2.6× the absorption effect and the other
  way. Two independent biases land on the same parameters; only the difference
  measured here is attributable to absorption.

**WP-0508 flat plate: the same module, and every conclusion inverts.** ITC
Table 6.3.3.1 case (2) (finite-thickness reflection) and case (3a) (symmetric
transmission), both validated against adaptive quadrature of the defining
path-length integral to < 1e-9 over 0 ≤ µt ≤ 5 and 5–140° 2θ. Unlike the
cylinder they are **not** exact reparameterisations — 1–40 % of ln A survives
projection onto {1, sin²θ} — so they move Rwp, their ΔBiso is an order of
magnitude larger (**−1.5 Å² at µt = 0.2** over a Cu Kα range) and *negative*: a
thin specimen runs out of material where the beam penetrates deepest, and the
missing high-angle intensity reads as thermal motion.

The real-data result for flat plate is a **negative** one, and it is the right
one: every flat-plate pattern in the repo is a thick back-packed mount, for
which ITC (1a) gives an exactly angle-independent A — the assumption this
package already made. Declaring a thickness where there is none is visible as
*worse* fit: on round-robin fluorite, µt = 0.5 takes Rwp 0.1793 → 0.1830 and
drives one Biso onto its bound. So for this correction the agent rule inverts:
a worse Rwp after declaring a thickness is evidence the specimen was not thin
([AGENT_PROTOCOL §8.12](../AGENT_PROTOCOL.md)).

**And the flat-plate ΔBiso is a lower bound, which only an end-to-end test
showed.** Generating a pattern *through* the correction from a known B = 0.8 and
refitting without declaring the thickness recovers B exactly when it is declared
(5e-4) and inflates it by 1.8 Å² at µt = 0.15 when it is not — but the reported
bias understates that, systematically, by a factor that tracks
`unabsorbed_fraction`:

| µt | 0.15 | 0.3 | 0.6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| unabsorbed fraction | 0.263 | 0.201 | 0.080 |
| actual / predicted bias | ~1.5 | ~1.3 | ~1.06 |

The projection behind the prediction is unweighted while a refinement finds a
weighted compromise, and the two agree only insofar as the correction really is
a {scale, Biso} direction — exactly for the cylinder, approximately here. So the
flat-plate number is quoted as "at least this much" with the residue beside it,
and the relationship is pinned by a test rather than asserted in prose.

Neither µR nor µt is refinable, and the *grounds* differ, which the record keeps
rather than smooths: µR's derivative lies identically in span{1, sin²θ} (an
exactly singular direction), while µt's keeps 3–47 % of its signature and is
merely ill-conditioned. µt is held fixed because it is knowable from the
specimen, because a free one lands in the {scale, Biso, background} corner, and
because what it would silently re-apportion is the ADPs the correction exists to
protect. `mu_t_identifiable_fraction` reports the number so a caller can
disagree.

## WP-0504 anomalous f′/f″ — the largest measured accuracy win in the milestone

`Source.dispersion` applies f′ + i·f″ from a bundled Cromer-Liberman table.
The load-bearing piece is not that f goes complex — F always was — but that a
powder measures the **Friedel average**: `generate_reflections` merges ±h into
one Laue orbit and evaluates a single representative, exact only while f is
real. The closed form ⟨|F|²⟩ = |A|² + |B|² (A carrying f₀+f′, B carrying f″,
over the *same* orbit sums) needs no second orbit pass and no centro/non-centro
split, and reduces bit-identically when the block is absent.

Refitting the eight IUCr round-robin sample-1 mixtures under the identical v0.3
protocol:

| | v0.3 (no dispersion) | with f′, f″ |
|---|---|---|
| RMS \|ΔW\| | 2.26 wt % | **0.69 wt %** |
| worst \|ΔW\| | 5.13 wt % | **1.39 wt %** |

This **re-derives a v0.3 conclusion**: the signed bias v0.3 had attributed to
microabsorption is mostly neglected dispersion. The giveaway was fluorite coming
back *high*, which microabsorption cannot explain. WP-0502's negative result
(below) is what makes that attribution clean rather than one of two candidates.

Its sharpest single result is elsewhere: on pure ZnO, Rwp barely moves while
**B(O) goes from 0.022 to 0.429 Å²** — a displacement parameter that had been
spent absorbing Zn's missing f′.

The default stays **off**, so every previously shipped acceptance number remains
valid; flipping it is a re-measurement of the whole validation matrix
([WP-1001](../wp/1001-validation-matrix.md)).

## WP-0502 surface roughness — a negative result, and the fences are what passed

`Geometry.surface_roughness` carries two published models behind a `kind` seam
(Suortti 1972, Pitschke 1993), exactly the identity when off. Both were verified
against primary sources *before* coding, which killed two of the WP's own draft
claims: Pitschke's P₀ is dropped (angle-independent ⇒ exactly degenerate with
the phase scale, and the paper never resolved it either), and Suortti's `b`
turned out to be **bimodal** — both b → 0 and b → ∞ are the identity — so its
bound, stage seed and fence come from measured sensitivity rather than
convention.

**No dataset in the repo can constrain a low-angle intensity correction.**
Measured: qarr corundum / fluorite / zincite first reflect at 25.6 / 28.3 /
31.8°, SRM 660c at 21.4°. Two of the three qarr phases drive the correction back
to the identity and raise `ROUGHNESS_UNCONSTRAINED`; the third slides along the
roughness↔Biso degenerate direction for **0.0001 in Rwp** with ρ(a,b) = +1.000
and esds 350× the values. Roughness is therefore *not* a competing explanation
for the sample-1 QPA bias.

Two methodological results outlived the WP:

* **A multiplicative correction is trivially ~0.96 "scale-like."**
  `optimize.statistics.block_projection_r2` takes a **nuisance** argument for
  this reason — project the scale and background out first and read the
  *partial* R², which tracks identifiability properly (0.06 → 0.95 as the
  low-angle reflections leave the fitted range).
* **Judge a correction at the reflections, not on the fitted grid.** The
  round-robin patterns start at 5° 2θ; a grid-based fence reported a 27 %
  depression no modelled peak ever saw.

## WP-0503 Stephens anisotropic strain — an improvement that is still rejected

`Phase.microstrain` is an optional block of the fifteen S_HKL invariants, with
the Laue-allowed subspace **derived** from the gemmi operators by exact rational
algebra rather than tabulated — the DOF counts reproduce Stephens' Table 1 for
all eleven Laue classes, checked twice (against the published table and against
the character count of the degree-4 symmetric power). The Layer-1 diagnostic
recovers an injected 3.46× directional contrast as **3.45×**.

The acceptance is a **characterisation**, not a win. On round-robin brucite the
three added patterns improve Rwp 18.55 → 17.90 % with **ΔBIC +488** — and still
leave the physical cone, so `STEPHENS_STRAIN_NOT_POSITIVE` fires and the
coefficients are not quotable. Corundum is the isotropic control: ΔBIC −17, the
directional diagnostic 1.60×, not detected.

Two findings went downstream: the cone is a *linear* inequality a constrained
solver could enforce ([WP-0601](../wp/0601-bounded-lm-solver.md)), and
Hamilton's R-ratio test is useless at 7251 channels — it blesses an inert 0.13 %
χ² improvement — so **ΔBIC is the statistic to quote**
([WP-1001](../wp/1001-validation-matrix.md)).

## WP-0505 sequential series — two defaults changed, one design assumption refuted

`SequentialRefinement` / `refine_sequential` chains N refinements over an ordered
series, each warm-started from its predecessor, returning per-pattern summaries
plus parameter *trajectories* (state, not curves — the rule the history nodes
follow). One history tree per pattern, pinned to its data by
`TreeHeader.data_fingerprint`.

On the eight round-robin sample-1 mixtures, under the v0.3 QPA protocol imported
wholesale so that only the *chaining* differs:

| strategy | iterations | mean Rwp | QPA accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| unchained (v0.3 baseline) | 2863 | 0.1278 | RMS 2.26 wt %, worst 5.13 |
| chained, staged plan re-walked warm | 1623 | 0.1278 | identical |
| chained, plan collapsed to one stage | **904** | 0.1278 | identical |

So `refit="single"` is the default: the staged turn-on order exists to keep
early stages conditioned from a *poor* starting model, and a converged
neighbour is not one. And the `carry`-glob hypothesis — that chaining a phase
scale across a 1 → 94 wt % swing would be worse than starting cold — is
**false**: carrying everything costs 838 iterations against 904 for a carry that
excludes the scales.

Three findings carried forward:

* **A smooth trajectory is exactly what a poisoned chain produces.**
  `direction="both"` runs the series each way and reports
  `SEQUENTIAL_PATH_DEPENDENT` per parameter — the only check that separates a
  measured trajectory from an ordering artefact.
* **Ratio-based fences need a noise floor, and the σ leg cannot supply it.** A
  softplus coefficient dying on its floor has dp/du → 0, so its esd collapses
  *alongside* its value and the significance test inverts: an unrefinable
  `instrument.profile.y` came back with σ ≈ 4e-55 and two chains "disagreeing"
  at 1e16 σ over 1e-60 vs 1e-74. Both fences now also require the step to be
  1e-9 of the parameter's own magnitude.
* **The reseed fence never fired on the hostile series** — the collapsed refit
  recovers a bad warm start *within* the fit, so the cold restart is insurance,
  not a routine mechanism.

## WP-0506 secondary extinction, WP-0507 anode wavelengths

**0506** implements the Sabine (1985/1988) polycrystalline blend, evaluated
unconditionally so ext = 0 is bit-identically the identity. Its acceptance is a
does-no-harm one on a NIST line-profile standard, where genuine extinction is
negligible: freeing it on SRM 660c refines it back *below* its 1e-3 seed
(≈ 2e-10) without moving the cell out of the v0.2 band. Extinction↔scale is a
genuine ρ ≈ 0.97 degeneracy — reconciled to the physics in WP-0407 rather than
silenced.

**0507** adds Cr, Fe, Co, Mo and Ag to `CuKa`, each also as a Kα1-only variant.
The table is four lines; the work was the two questions around it.

*Which scale.* All six come from one column of one evaluation — the NIST X-ray
Transition Energies Database (SRD 128), direct-experimental KL3/KL2 — and the
argument for trusting it is internal rather than bibliographic: **that column's
Cu pair is bit-identical to the `CuKa` values shipped since v0.2**, so the
existing entry is unchanged and doubles as the proof the new rows share its
scale. A test asserts it, including the negative half (≠ Bearden's 1.540562,
24–26 ppm away at Mo/Ag).

*What silently assumed Cu.* `background.diagnostics._contamination_flags`
returned `[]` for any wavelength more than 0.01 Å from Cu Kα1 — so every new
anode would have reported `contamination == []`, which reads as *clean* rather
than *not checked*. It is now per anode, with W Lα1 checked for every anode
(it comes off the **filament**) and Kβ only for a recognised one (it comes off
the target). And the 26.6° graphite monochromator angle in the `bragg_brentano`
docstring is a *Cu* number: the same graphite sits at 12.1° at Mo Kα, where the
polarization constant K is 0.511 rather than 0.500.

One measured finding worth the milestone: **the one-|F|²-per-source assumption
is weaker off Cu.** The Kα1/Kα2 gap grows from 20 eV at Cu to 173 eV at Ag, and
a census over Z = 3–98 × six anodes finds `dispersion.resolve` refusing **7 of
576** combinations — nothing at all at Cr and Fe, and at Ag one specimen someone
will actually mount: **Ru, K edge 22.14 keV, right between the lines**. The
census is pinned as a test so a table or tolerance change has to restate it.

## Repo-wide audit, 2026-07-28 (not a WP)

Run alongside the milestone at the user's request. It found and root-caused one
real bug: `np.linalg.pinv` was taking its general SVD path on JᵀJ, losing
symmetry on the cond ≈ 10²⁰ normal matrices this package routinely forms and
emitting |ρ| up to **1.6 × 10³**. `hermitian=True` on a symmetrised matrix caps
it at 1 + 4 ulp, so correlation reporting — and the 0.98 guard that both 0501
and 0502 lean on — measures the data's degeneracies again rather than the linear
algebra's.

It also settled WP-0501's b₂ question without a clean copy of the paper (an
independently written quadrature of ITC (6.3.3.4) reproduces Rouse's own 0.0035
bound with −0.3750 and 0.0820 with −0.0375), and shipped two new deliverables:
[AGENT_PROTOCOL.md](../AGENT_PROTOCOL.md) and `pxrdref compare`, the
settings-comparison UI whose standards are pinned to the acceptance suites'
protocols by `tests/test_compare_ui.py`.

The physics itself was checked module by module against its cited sources and
**no errors were found**: FCJ's cos 2φ relation and ξ_max cap, the TCH and Voigt
closed-form partials, the Friedel-average identity, the Stephens
Λ = (180/π)·10⁻⁶·d²√(ΣS·mono) chain, Sabine's branches, Brindley's τ series and
the Rouse ΔB = c·λ²/2 bias all reproduce independently.

## What v0.5 says about how to judge a correction

Collecting the eight rows, because the pattern is the milestone's actual result:

| Correction | Δ Rwp | What it actually changes |
|---|---|---|
| capillary absorption | **provably zero** (measured 3e-8) | Biso, by +0.0167 Å² at µR = 0.674 / +0.489 at µR = 1 |
| flat-plate absorption | moves it — *upward* on a thick specimen | Biso, by up to −1.5 Å², and it is the sign nobody expects |
| anomalous f′/f″ | barely moves it | QPA fractions (RMS 2.26 → 0.69 wt %) and ADPs (B(O) 0.02 → 0.43 Å²) |
| surface roughness | 0.0001 | nothing measurable here — the fences fire instead |
| Stephens strain | 18.55 → 17.90 %, ΔBIC +488 | nothing quotable: the positivity cone rejects it |
| secondary extinction | ~0 on a fine powder | nothing — and refining it back to 2e-10 is the result |
| anode wavelengths | n/a | which specimens are refusable at all (7 of 576) |
| sequential chaining | identical | 2863 → 904 iterations at identical accuracy |

**Not one of the eight is well judged by Δ Rwp.** Two cannot move it, one moves
it the wrong way when it is right, three move it while changing nothing
quotable, and the two largest accuracy wins in the milestone (dispersion on QPA,
absorption on ADPs) are both invisible in it. That is why every correction here
ships with a record field or a diagnostic that says what it did —
`result.absorption`, `ROUGHNESS_UNCONSTRAINED`, `STEPHENS_STRAIN_NOT_POSITIVE`,
`DISPERSION_NEGLECTED`, `ABSORPTION_THICKNESS_MATTERS` — and why
[AGENT_PROTOCOL §8.1](../AGENT_PROTOCOL.md) leads with it.
