# v1.11 — an indexing candidate is validated by a fit

**Cut 2026-11-04** (version → `1.11.0`), eleven days after 1.10.0
([notes](../releases/1.11.0.md)). This is the record of release 1.11: the
narrative of everything that landed on `main` before the cut, and the measured
ship block in the appendix (WP-1540). A milestone's own scope and acceptance
live in its named record
([magnetic.md](magnetic.md) and [generality.md](generality.md) for the two
open now).

## Milestones this release completes

None. The rows of both open milestones, magnetic and generality, are still
unmet. The release carries 183 merged pull requests, among them the indexer's
Pawley validation, WP-2003's series with no thresholds and WP-2086's noise check.

## How v1.11 is getting here

### A result names its radiation (2042, closed 2026-10-24)

A fitted result records the radiation it was fitted with, as `RefinementResult.radiation`: the
source's kind and each emission line's wavelength at the answer. So `result.plot()` labels its
2θ axis as `data.plot()` does, with nothing passed: `Cu Kα, λ = 1.54059 Å` for a tube preset,
and λ alone for a synchrotron, a neutron source or a refined wavelength. A joint fit records
each pattern's own on `HistogramResult.radiation`, which `for_histogram` swaps in. Without it
the per-pattern view would have labelled the second pattern with the first one's λ. A result
saved earlier reads `None`, "not recorded", and draws the figure it always drew, byte for byte.
Schema 0.59 → 0.60. It merged (#1152) after the 1.10.0 tag, so it ships in 1.11; WP-2043 moved
this section here from the v1.10 record.

and removed limits, the purposes WP-2037 prints diagnostics by, and the new

every test while its job ran six seconds past its 45-minute limit, and the
maintainer took the suite's result as the gate. The `full` nightly finished on

red (WP-2036). 1.10 is the first release to carry its own manual, now
`stable` at `/docs/`. The measured ship block is the
[v1.10 record](v1.10.md)'s appendix.

### A result reproduces its own number (1421, closed 2026-10-25)

A result now describes one state wherever it is read. Since #432 a single fit's curves and
statistics came from a compile at its answer; a joint fit's now do too, so its per-pattern report
no longer reads peak tails cut at stage-start windows as unmatched peaks, and
`FROZEN_COMPILE_STALE` tests each pattern and names the worst. `report()`, the reflection table,
`predict()` and the CIF writer read the answer saved with the result, so a cancelled later fit
cannot mix into it. The finding's claim that the fit is unbiased was withdrawn on two measured
counterexamples. The session re-measured the gap WP-1986 handed over, a plan stopping 9 % above
its re-solved χ², and found it closed for plans by WP-2002's closing stage; a hand-run stage keeps
it, which is WP-2047's subject. Issue #774's zero case was ruled documented, after a practice
survey and a probe showing the alternative doubles a default fit's time for a 4.8e-5 effect;
it is re-checked when WP-2047 lands.

### A stage that ends on stale windows says so (2047, closed 2026-10-25)

A stage computes each peak over a window sized where the stage starts, so a peak that moves or
broadens while it solves spills past it, and the solver can fit the lost intensity instead of the
data. That is how the CPD chain once ran its axial divergence from 0.04 to 558. Every stage now
records the share of the Bragg intensity its windows dropped at its answer, measured without a
compile at 3-5 % of a fit's time, and `STAGE_WINDOWS_STALE` warns when the stage whose answer
reaches the result drops more than its own window tolerance. The four acceptance standards stay
at or under 1.7 % against 2 %; the old runaway reads 73 %. A hand-run stage now reports its own
gap, which WP-1421 handed here, though nothing repairs it. Recompiling between solves was measured
and dropped: a check at a solve's end rebuilt windows at the runaway, and a check every few
iterations worked only at an interval fitted to one case. Windows resized at every evaluation, as
GSAS-II, MAUD, RIETAN-FP and EasyDiffraction do, removed the runaway at 1.24-2.08× the fit time
with no accuracy gain on the four standards. At the maintainer's request WP-2051 tests that design
with a faster resizing and a tapered window edge, and issue #774's zero case moved there with it.

### Windows resized at every evaluation, tested and filed (2051, closed 2026-10-25)

WP-2047's prototype resized each peak's window before every evaluation and cost 1.24-2.08× the
fit time. Most of that cost was one bisection for the window's half-width in FWHM units, solved
afresh per phase and emission line at a fixed ~0.36 ms. A table built once per tolerance replaced
it inside the resizing, which now takes 0.08-0.16 ms, so the ratio fell to 1.05-1.49. What
remained was extra solver evaluations, because the cost steps when a window edge crosses a data
point. A taper that fades each peak to zero over a tenth of its window, emulated in a probe,
recovered most of them, and built into the forward model it is inferred at 0.94-1.10× shipped.
Answers polished at tight windows came out the same as shipped's on the four standards, and the
old runaway stayed removed. The maintainer ruled to file it for now and keep it as a future
possibility, since it buys no accuracy and would rewrite the frozen-per-stage invariant on every
backend. The faster sizing ships, used by the stale-window measurement. The WP file says what
would reopen it and where to start.

### The indexer judges a candidate on a fit that converged (2011, closed 2026-10-29)

The indexer's whole-pattern check fitted each candidate with one Le Bail pass. Le Bail freezes
its extracted intensities inside each solve, so one pass stalled on true cells. On the
round-robin corundum and zircon patterns it left the Gaussian width `w` on its lower bound, with
21 and 44 observed peaks unmatched. A wrong cell could also lower its background below the truth
and read 0 absent lines. The check now fits by Pawley, which refines the intensities in the same
solve. The same two true cells hit no bound and leave 12 and 11 peaks unmatched. Magnetite's
primitive description reads Rwp 0.174 against the true F cell's 0.180, where one Le Bail pass
read 0.65 against 0.21. The first go was withheld on 2026-10-28, because under `quick` Pawley
validated none of NAC's candidates. The time kept for validation was being spent before
validation began, on ranking work after the search's clock fired (WP-2063 owns that). Under the
maintainer's delegation the session decided the go: validation fits now run cheapest first on
the run's clock, and the reserve went from 8 % to 15 %. NAC's true cell validates under `quick`.
On NAC's default spec the orthorhombic system is no longer reached. A sparse, preconditioned step
solve took the slowest pseudo-symmetric Pawley fit from 376 s to 34 s with no reading moved on
15 cells. The dense path gained a shifted Cholesky preconditioner and a projected search (Lin &
Moré 1999). WP-2062 inherits the remaining Pawley costs and the rename of
`validate_by_lebail`. PR #1191.

### A phase with no reflection in range keeps its scale (1958, closed 2026-10-31)

Under `solver="trf"` a phase whose fitted range held none of its reflections walked its scale to
164.7 and took the iron fraction to 99.99 wt%. Under the default LM the scale stayed at 1e-4 but QPA
printed the start value, 0.61 wt%. Issue #954's reporter asked for both halves, and the maintainer
ruled both on 2026-10-16. Such a phase now keeps the scale it was given under either solver, in a
single or a joint fit, and has no weight fraction. The other fractions are relative to the phases
seen in range, and the finding, the QPA table and the text summary say so. The three seen phases on
the repro now carry esds, because the dead column no longer sits in their sum. Brindley's correction
is skipped with its reason while a phase is unmeasured, since it needs every fraction. PR #1103 shipped
in 1.10.0. Its follow-ups, PR #1176, ship in 1.11: a flag on the QPA trajectory for a change of basis, a
held scale driven by a variable named as one, joint scales tied to one variable, and the missing-radius
advice.

### The satellite arm generates over the model's window (2034, closed 2026-10-31)

The report's satellite arm kept its own copy of the angular window `compile_model` generates
reflections over, and the copy had no margin, capped at 179° and applied the low cutoff in the wrong
emission line's frame. On a Cu Kα doublet a satellite whose Kα1 image fell just under the range start

### A body on a special position (1807, closed 2026-10-31)

A rigid body on a special position now takes only the rotations and origin shifts its site stabiliser
allows, through `wyckoff.axial_basis`, which equals the moment law without time reversal on all 136
point-group subgroups of m-3m and 6/mmm, and is 0, 1 or 3 dimensional on each. Diacetylene on the Pnma
mirror builds with two origin DOFs and one rotation DOF, and a body on the C2/c two-fold stays on its axis
as the cell moves. Issue #876 found the opposite failure: the old refusal's remedy, a move off the mirror,
built and predicted a doubled composition (S 8, O 32 per PbSO4 cell against 4 and 16) with nothing firing.
`RIGID_BODY_IMAGE_OVERLAP` now refuses a body whose atoms straddle an element, summing the occupancy of
every image of a place so a three- or six-fold site is held to one atom. A disorder group exempts a body
that is deliberately disordered. PRs #841 and #1005, and the follow-ups #1226 (a cross-backend row on
HKUST-1's linker, and the site-miss factor named and set at 8). The overlap check is O(live² · |G|) in
Python and unmeasured on a realistic body.

### Magnetic superspace groups from the operators (1992, closed 2026-11-01)

`crystallography/superspace/` generates the (3+1)D magnetic superspace group (MSSG) of each irrep
at a real propagation vector. A modulated structure has atoms displaced by a wave that does not
repeat with the cell, and the group needs one extra dimension for the wave's phase. A magnetic
group adds time reversal, written θ on the operator and {1′|000½} for the pure translation
in the extra dimension. The package stays unexported, so no user sees it until the magnetic forward
model lands on it. The group of an irrep comes from a declared line (`little_group_on_line`),
because spgrep's little group is exact at a rational k but snaps a near-rational k below
|Rᵀk − k| ≈ 1e-8 and declines `real=True` on 6 of 9 test lines. spgrep stays a dev-only oracle.
The pins are Stokes & Campbell (2022) Tables 1-3 and Fig. 4, Perez-Mato (2012) Table 1, and the six
MSSGs of SrFeO₃ (Gallego 2016). Types 1-3 match the published counts (775, 775, 3100). Type 4
gives 4651 against the printed 4653, a strict xfail that stays open. It merged from outside as
#1060 (`44de0f62`), and #1227 (`c6c75653`) added a control showing `LINE_ATOL` does not move with
λ near a rational. Yue ruled to close the WP with that xfail standing.

### A peak list refuses a q_esd its two_theta_esd does not give (2071, closed 2026-11-01)

A peak list stores each line's uncertainty twice, as σ(2θ) and as σ(Q), and the indexer weights
lines by σ(Q). The list already refused a Q that its 2θ did not give, but nothing tied the two
uncertainties. So an edit to one line's σ(2θ) loaded clean and never reached the search, and
#1236's weighting experiment ran an arm that changed nothing. The list now checks σ(Q) against
σ(2θ) in the same pass, at a relative 1e-9 with no absolute floor, and a pair where both are
non-finite agrees. The refusal names the peak and both numbers, and says to set both fields
together. Every package constructor derives σ(Q) through the same function, and has since the
peak editor landed, so no saved list from a shipped release is affected. Reported from outside
by @mustachefeeling, fixed in #1281.

### The moment in a series (1329, closed 2026-11-01)

A temperature series through a magnetic ordering transition now refines the moment on every pattern the
data support, holds it at its floor above the onset, and reports |m| against temperature with esds, the
held patterns marked and carrying none. Each direction of the chain records its own onset, and a flag
fires when the two disagree (PRs #522 and #589). The last task, a real ramp, landed as PR #1073: twelve
of the 23 corrected LLB G4.1 neutron patterns of Ba2FeSbSe5 (CC BY 4.0, released by the co-author), the
first k ≠ 0 series in the tree. Both chains bracket the onset at 50 to 55 K, 3 K under the bulk T_N of
58 K, and the moment at 55 K is only 1.9 times its esd, so an onset read from the verdicts sits under T_N.
The 1.5 K moment is 3.875 ± 0.131 μB forward and 3.882 ± 0.124 μB backward, against the paper's 4.13(4)
under another code. The fit ties the 28 child Biso to their 7 parent sites, because untied the Fe Biso ran
from −8.8 to +23.6 Å2, and the tie costs Rwp 5.21 to 6.13 % at 1.5 K, a misfit still unidentified.
Three notes the WP could not finish moved to WP-1327: a joint fit does not hold a per-histogram moment as
flat, the fences read a powder twin as path dependence, and the two series types have no provisional
declaration a test can see.

### Tether, planarity and anti-bump restraints (1809, closed 2026-11-01)

Three restraint kinds joined bond, angle and value. A tether is a one-sided maximum distance, zero inside
and slope 1/σ outside. An anti-bump row is a one-sided minimum over a pair list the caller builds once
(`anti_bump_restraints`), so the restraint row count never changes between stages; that replaced the
engine-frozen list the design record first described. A planarity restraint is one row per atom, its
distance from a least-squares plane whose normal is the scatter matrix's smallest-eigenvalue eigenvector
(Schomaker et al. 1959), refused when the two smallest eigenvalues are degenerate. A contact that comes
inside its minimum from outside the list is named at plan end by `RESTRAINT_PAIRS_STALE`, a warning for
fits whose numbers depend on coordinates and `info` for a cell, phase-identification, microstructure or
calibration fit. The search runs only where a coordinate or cell parameter moved, about 46 ms at the end of
a 0.81 s fit on a 20-atom body. The maintainer ruled that the builder keeps the caller-stated minimum;
reading radii tables for a default stays a later convenience under WP-1515. PRs #888, #995 and #1222, from
outside.

### A non-finite step ends a stage, not the fit (2048, closed 2026-11-01)

The nightly's triclinic Le Bail row raised `ValueError: array must not contain infs or NaNs` out of
`Refinement.fit` on macOS arm64, from a start nudged 1e-14 relative. Under `solver="trf"` no floor holds the
peak widths, so once Y goes negative the TCHZ Γ⁵ term does too and the model turns non-finite. SciPy's TRF
shortens a step whose residual is NaN, but raises on a NaN Jacobian and on a non-finite start, and so does the
LM driver. Both solver entry points now check the starting residual and every Jacobian. A stop hands back the
solve's own start as `diverged`, the stage puts every value back where it began, the plan carries on, and
`STAGE_NOT_FINITE` (a warning) names the stage, what went non-finite and the free set. Of six nudged starts,
four raised before and all six return a finite Rwp now. Ending at the start rather than at the last finite
iterate follows the cell-runaway reset (WP-1528) and the collapse restore (WP-2002). PR #1292.

### A reduced cell is its input's exact image (2035, closed 2026-11-01)

`reduce_cell` returned gemmi's ε-reduced G6 vector wherever no sign of it disagreed with the image of
the input, and that vector is not the image of anything. Within ε of a right angle gemmi's `normalize`
sets the sign of a near-zero parameter without recording a change of basis, and the later Křivý-Gruber
steps carry the error. WP-1930's F-centred cell sat 1.1e-7 of max|G| off its image that way, and so did
about a third of primitive cells drawn one angle 1e-5° from 90°. Since PR #1136 (from outside, by
@mustachefeeling) every reduced cell is built from M·G·Mᵀ; the unit replay digests and the indexing
acceptance suite did not move. The same drift then made the property test flaky: it still compared the
cell with gemmi's vector to within ε, and that vector sits up to 1.65 ε (P) and 1.99 ε (I) off the image,
so about one CI run in 40 went red (issue #1252). PR #1300 judges the cell by the Niggli conditions at ε
instead (Gruber 1973 eq. 3, Křivý & Gruber 1976 steps 5-8, ε placed as gemmi places it), which no reduced
cell of 200 000 near-right draws over five centrings breaks. It pins two of the issue's cells, WP-2011's
case and the 1.99 ε I-centred draw.

### Maximal subgroups and Wyckoff splittings from the operators (1919, closed 2026-11-01)

`rietx.crystallography.representation.subgroups` now generates the maximal subgroups of a space
group, and the splitting of each Wyckoff position under one, from the group's own operators and
nothing printed. `maximal(group, kind, max_index)` returns every maximal t- and k-subgroup up to
index 4, which covers every non-isomorphic one, and an isomorphic subgroup on demand by `index=`
or `supercell=`. Each carries its conjugacy class and class size, and its (P, p) to the standard
setting checked in exact `Fraction` arithmetic against the Hall-database group. `wyckoff_positions`
derives the 1731 positions of the 230 types, and `wyckoff_splitting` states the (P, p) its labels
belong to. The reporter's prototype (#806) was merged from outside in two PRs, #1189 (the
generator, 2026-10-30) and #1239 (the positions and the splitting, 2026-11-01). PR #1248 had
`magnetic/isotropy.py`'s `_standard_child` call the same exact standardiser, so the package holds
one. Seventeen ITA A1 entries read off the page are the reference set, each found up to the
affine normaliser. The index ≤ 4 sweep over all 230 types stayed in the fast suite, against the
ruling that it be `slow`, at about 11 s at `-n 4`. The index ≤ 9 sweep is
`tests/subgroups_benchmark.py`. WP-1418's descent audit, WP-1515's step 7 and WP-2069 are its
consumers, none built.

### The picker offers no line the data cannot support (1968, closed 2026-11-01)

Issue #934 found `pick_peaks` offering every indexing engine lines the data could not support:
re-seeds on peak tails, and splits of one line into several. Five PRs, all from outside,

### A frame no phase is seen in holds the instrument's peak terms too (2049, closed 2026-11-01)

The planted series' blank frame 87 could refine its zero shift and widths against nothing. The
support hold (WP-1301) held an unseen phase's cell, and never an instrument column, because one
seen phase gives a shared column real gradient. With every phase unseen that argument has no
force. Fitted alone under `mccusker_default`, frame 87 came back with a zero shift of
−0.0129 ± 0.0015° against a truth of 0.006°, and the 86-88 chain quoted −0.0229 ± 0.0039° at it.
Now, when every phase is still unseen at a stage's answer, the stage puts back and holds each free
instrument column whose reach the pattern reads only through phase peaks, and the next stage holds
it from its start. A hold at the start on a reading under 3σ would have cost a weak real phase the
zero shift it needs to be seen. `CompiledModel.scalar_chain_supported` decides which, with
the two FCJ axial ratios added. The background and every extra component stay free. A declared
sharp peak keeps the polarisation and the line weights free too. The release lifts the columns once
any phase is seen. A joint fit holds a blank histogram's own zero shift. A series carries the last
measured value across the blank frame. `PHASE_UNCONSTRAINED` names the held rows. A start 1-3 FWHM
off in zero shift recovers exactly as before. Frame 87 now costs 56 iterations against 111.
PR #1305.

### A fit keeps each site's multiplicity from start to answer (2052, closed 2026-11-01)

Issue #1088 found a fit halving a site it had just fitted. A half-occupied general atom is the
usual way to write a split pair across a mirror. Where the data cannot resolve the split, the cost
is even in the distance off the mirror, so the solver stops on it, and the next compile counted
the site at the mirror's multiplicity with the occupancy unchanged. On the issue's Pnma toy,
noise-free under the default plan, that returned 10 O per cell for 12, χ²_red 23.2 and O1 Biso
−1.64, with only `BISO_NEGATIVE` said. `fit()`, `run_stage()` and a joint fit now decide each
site's orbit split once, from the coordinates they start at, and every compile in the call reuses
it, the closing compile and the report's twin compiles included. GSAS-II reads a site's
multiplicity once, at setup, which is the practice the decision rests on (settled by Claude). The
same toy now returns χ²_red 1e-16, 12 O and Biso 0.600, and the 819-cell coordinate walk an
occupancy stage showed is gone. A compile never withholds images: a site that leaves a special
position, which only a rigid body's pose can do, takes its full orbit as before. The returned
coordinates still read as the special position to anything that compiles afresh, so
`SITE_MULTIPLICITY_KEPT` names such a site with its atoms per cell both ways. Between calls the
split is still decided afresh (a second `run_stage`, a series' next pattern, `replay`, the CIF
writer); WP-2053 holds that. PR #1307.

does, and a block declaring a frame input it does not read is refused.

### A result lists only what the fit could move (1999, closed 2026-11-01)

### An unmatched peak says whether it sits on a drawn flank (1977, closed 2026-11-02)

The fit report listed a residual peak as unindexed whenever no tick lay within 0.08°. After WP-1908 put the
ticks on the peaks, the misfit lobes a shape or asymmetry error leaves 0.1-0.4° below strong lines fell
outside that window, and two of them drove an "add an impurity phase" suggestion on fits of known
single-phase or weighed specimens (issue #1106 point 3). A first design dropped any residual peak the
observed curve joins to a drawn peak with no 5σ dip. The generality review found that such a shoulder is a
question the data cannot settle: a shape misfit, an unmodelled Kα2, a lower-symmetry split and a foreign
line all leave one. The maintainer ruled on 2026-11-01 to leave the call to the reader. Every row stays
listed, and each `unmatched_obs` row carries `UnmatchedPeak.flank`: whether the data resolve it from the
drawn peak, that peak's position, prominence and width, the offset in its FWHM, and how many lines as strong
carry a lobe on the same side. `add_impurity_phase` no longer counts a row on a flank, and `rietx.help`
explains what separates the four causes. Across the twelve QPA round-robin fits and SRM 660c, 66 of 116
rows are on a flank, 0.7-3.0 FWHM from their line on every fit that converged. `add_impurity_phase` left cpd-1b and SRM 660c with the apertures at 0,
and stayed on cpd-4, whose lines the data resolve. A blank's line counts as a drawn peak (#1217). A
tetragonal LaB₆ modelled cubic keeps its re-index pointer. Report thresholds 1.13 → 1.14. PR #1295.
