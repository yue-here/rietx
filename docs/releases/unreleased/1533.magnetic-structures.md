- `displacive_statement(parent, candidate=c)` states a child straight from a `distortion_candidates` entry (WP-1419).
  The driving irrep's modes come first, and the seed lies in that irrep alone, whose isotropy group is the child's.
  A refused candidate, one whose isotropy group failed verification, and one built from another parent are refused by name.
  At the parent, with every non-identity amplitude and every strain at zero, a stage that frees only strains is refused, as one freeing only amplitudes already was.
  The refusal is about the start: the gradient there is exactly zero, or set only by what else breaks the parent's symmetry (moments, preferred orientation, unequal ADPs or occupancies), which has not been shown to choose the sign reliably, so the caller states the sign by seeding.
- A row `displacive_statement` derives is returned held, and a free flag the child stated on it is recorded rather than dropped (Michael's ruling D59).
  The derived rows are the six cell parameters under `Phase.parent_cell` and a mode-moved atom's x, y and z.
  `DisplaciveStatement.held` pairs each reset row with the paths that now refine it, and `DisplaciveStatement.diagnostics` carries one `info` row, `CHILD_VARY_RESET`, naming them; no warning, since the GSAS and GSAS-II importers state every refined child's cell free.
  `ParameterRow.carried_by` gives a derived cell row the same paths, so its `held_because` names the parent's edges and strains rather than a symmetry.
  A child whose parent is stated by an operator list keeps its own cell flags.
