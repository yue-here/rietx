# 7j. The magnetic family: a satellite, a stated moment and the child group it implies

Load it when a magnetic `Diagnostic` fired — from `analyse_moments` on a fit's `FitReport.magnetic` rows, from `magnetic_supercell`'s returned statement, or from a magCIF/TOPAS/FullProf import — when you were handed a magnetic structure to read, or before you read `FitReport.satellites`.

*A reference file of the `rietx` skill. The body it belongs to is [`SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md); section numbers are the ones the body cites.*

The magnetic diagnostic family lives in this one file rather than in §7's tables (`references/diagnostics.md`; maintainer ruling, issue #286): one order-parameter question, one file. **The channel varies by table**, and each row says which:

* **Moment codes** are about a refined moment (WP-1327) and ride on the report's `MomentEvidence` rows (`analyse_moments`, `FitReport.magnetic`).
* **Candidate codes** are not on `result.diagnostics` at all: they are on the `SupercellStatement` `magnetic_supercell` returns, because the question — does a Hermann-Mauguin symbol name the child group this cell carries — is settled before a fit exists, the same way a project reader's own codes are.
* The **satellite arm** (WP-1326, `FitReport.satellites`) emits no code at all; its readings are in the next section.
* **Interchange codes** are about a magnetic structure read from a file (WP-1328) and fire at *import* — pass `diagnostics=[]` to the reader to collect them; they never reach `result.diagnostics`.

## The satellite arm

| reading | what you must not assume, and what to do |
|---|---|
| `SatelliteCandidate.matched` at the top of `SatelliteEvidence.candidates` | Read the top row as the propagation vector. The count has no chance baseline: on the Cr₂WO₆ tutorial set the best zone-boundary k indexes 2 of 6 leftover peaks at 4 K and 2 of 5 at 150 K, where there is no order. Compare `matched` with `n_satellites` (a k with a line everywhere matches by being everywhere) and with the runner-up (the note quotes all three, and calls a tie), then declare the k (`Phase.propagation_vector`) and run a Le Bail or Pawley stage: whether the extraction puts intensity on the satellites is the measurement |
| `SatelliteEvidence.excess_on_absent_lattice_lines` > 0 | Read it as a k = 0 magnetic structure. The points are forbidden only under the group you assumed: a lower nuclear group, λ/2 contamination and an impurity line also put intensity there, and on X-rays magnetism is not a cause at all. Rule those out first; on neutrons, then test a moment model under a k = 0 magnetic space group (§7j above), not a propagation vector, which refuses k = 0 |
| `SatelliteEvidence.excess_on_nuclear_lines` > 0 | Call it magnetic or nuclear from this pattern. A k = 0 structure and a nuclear misfit look alike here; a pattern of the same specimen above its ordering temperature separates them |
| `SatelliteEvidence.radiation == "xray"` | Call intensity at G ± k magnetic. On X-rays it is a superstructure reflection |

## Moment codes

| code | what you must not assume, and what to do |
|---|---|
| `MOMENT_PAIR_DEGENERATE` | (info — from `report.magnetic.moment_pair_diagnostics` over `analyse_moments`' rows) Read either site's own `MomentEvidence.magnitude` for a paired row. The powder measures only the pair's quadrature sum, `sqrt(m_a^2 + m_b^2)` (`MomentEvidence.paired_magnitude` and `.paired_magnitude_esd`, `paired_with` naming the other site) — the two moduli are correlated in the fit and not separately determined, so either one's raw magnitude is an artefact of that correlation rather than a measurement |

## Candidate codes

| code | what you must not assume, and what to do |
|---|---|
| `CHILD_GROUP_UNNAMED` | (info — from `magnetic_supercell`, on the returned statement rather than on a `FitReport`; its `where` is `space_group`, relative to the returned phase) Read the child's `space_group` as a symbol. It is a **label**: the bracketed form `'Pm [unnamed in 2a,b,a+c]'` says that no Hermann-Mauguin symbol generates this group in this cell — a parent glide's ½ along a doubled axis is a ¼ in the child, which is in no tabulated operation list — so the leading symbol names the closest *type* and generates a different group. The group is `phase.symmetry_operations`, and every orbit, multiplicity and systematic absence comes from it; the cell's metric constraints come from the closest type's point group and lattice, which are this group's own. Quote the operation list, or the type **with the cell transform beside it**. Two things such a group does not have: a Wyckoff letter (`site_constraints(...).wyckoff` is empty) and a `SPACE_GROUP_SETTING_ASSUMED` warning, both being properties of a tabulated setting. A published symbol for the same superstructure is naming a type in some other cell: check the transform before comparing |

## Interchange codes

A magCIF's atom **positions** refine under the highest tabulated group the file's atoms satisfy — the parent it names where that contains its magnetic operators with time reversal dropped and keeps every listed site's orbit (`CIF_MAGNETIC_NUCLEAR_GROUP` says so, with the index over the file's own group), else the file's own family group (`CIF_MAGNETIC_NUCLEAR_SETTING`) — while the **moments** always refine under the file's magnetic group; read `phases[0].space_group` and those two codes to see which happened. Override with `Structure.from_cif(..., nuclear_group="file")` (always the file's own group) or `nuclear_group="parent"` (always the parent, refused by name where it cannot carry the file's atoms); the default is `"auto"` (issue #457).

| code | what you must not assume, and what to do |
|---|---|
| `CIF_MAGNETIC_ION_UNCHARGED` | (info — from `Structure.from_cif`, pass `diagnostics=[]` to collect it; it is not on `result.diagnostics`) Assume the magnetic form factor is the one your chemistry implies. The file states no oxidation state for a magnetic site — MAGNDATA writes a bare `Mn` for a site that is chemically Mn³⁺, and magCIF has no item for the ion at all — so the **neutral atom's** ⟨j₀⟩ was used. Not cosmetic: ⟨j₀⟩ for `Mn` and for `Mn3+` are different curves, so a moment refined under the wrong one absorbs the difference into its magnitude. `where` names the site; state it with `Structure.from_cif(..., moment_ions={"Mn1": "Mn3+"})` and the code stops firing. The `suggestion` lists the ions of that element the table does carry |
| `MAGNETIC_ION_ASSUMED` | (info — same reader, same channel) Assume the neutral-atom fallback above is what fired. It is not, here: this site's bare element has **no neutral-atom row at all** — every lanthanide and actinide this table carries has only ionic ⟨jₙ⟩ curves (Brown, ITC Vol C) — so the fallback went one step further, to the element's majority oxidation state that is *magnetic* (Ln³⁺, except Eu → Eu²⁺ since Eu³⁺ is 4f⁶/J=0; Yb³⁺; U⁴⁺, Np⁴⁺, Pu³⁺ among the actinides this table's rows cover; WP-1327 D1). This is a stronger substitution than the neutral-atom one, so it fires instead of `CIF_MAGNETIC_ION_UNCHARGED`, never alongside it. `where` names the site; state it with `moment_ions={"Dy1": "Dy3+"}` and the code stops firing — the same route silences either one |
| `LANDE_G_ASSUMED` | (info — same reader, same channel; only ever alongside `MAGNETIC_ION_ASSUMED` on the same site) Assume that resolving the ion above was the whole fix. It was not: every ion `MAGNETIC_ION_ASSUMED` can produce is a lanthanide or actinide needing an explicit Landé g (`needs_explicit_g`), and magCIF has no item for g any more than it has one for the ion — so without this default the site refused one line later, on `resolve_g`, and never loaded at all (WP-1327 D-lande-g). The free-ion **Hund's-rule g_J** was used instead — `value` carries it, derived from the assumed ion's (S, L, J) ground term via g_J = 1 + [J(J+1)+S(S+1)-L(L+1)]/[2J(J+1)] (Ashcroft & Mermin, ch. 31) — because the ion is already an assumption and g is a fixed atomic-physics number for it, not a second independent guess. **Never fires for a *stated* ion** (`moment_ions={"Dy1": "Dy3+"}`): there an absent g is the caller's own gap and `resolve_g` still refuses, unchanged (issue #257 A5). `where` names the site's `.moment.g`; state it with `moment_g={"Dy1": 1.333}` and the code stops firing |
| `CIF_MAGNETIC_SYMMFORM` | (info, or **warning** — same reader, same channel) Assume the file's `_atom_site_moment.symmform` is the constraint the refinement will use — it is not. rietx derives a moment's allowed subspace from the file's own **operator list** (the operators are the model), and `symmform` is the producer's record of the same thing. At `info` the two agree on the number of free parameters, which is evidence about the file that neither side gives alone. At **`warning`** they disagree, and that is the file's operator loop and this package's symmetry algebra saying different things about one site: check which, because the fit will follow the operators. `value` is the count `symmform` states, and the message quotes the derived one beside it |
| `CIF_MAGNETIC_UNIDENTIFIED` | (info — same reader, same channel) Assume `MagneticSymmetry.uni_number` is populated. The operator list did not resolve to one of spglib's 1651 magnetic space groups, so no UNI number was recorded. The **operator list is the model and is unaffected** — the number is metadata. Three of spglib 2.7.0's own database entries cannot be matched to themselves (UNI 282, 283, 284), so a non-standard setting or one of those three reaches here legitimately |
| `CIF_MAGNETIC_CENTERING_ASSUMED` | (info — same reader, same channel) Assume the file stated its magnetic group's centrings. It did not: it has no `_space_group_symop_magn_centering.xyz` loop, so the group is read as its operation list with the trivial centring `x,y,z,+1` alone. MAGNDATA always writes the loop, so this is a hand-written file. If the group has a centring or an anti-centring (`x,y,z+1/2,-1`), add the loop and read the file again, because without it the moments refine under a different magnetic group |
| `CIF_MAGNETIC_NUCLEAR_GROUP` | (info — same reader, same channel; fires on every magCIF read that keeps the parent) Assume the positions refine under the file's own symmetry. They refine under the **parent** the file names, which contained the file's magnetic operators with time reversal dropped and gave every listed site the same orbit, so it is the highest tabulated group the atoms satisfy. The message reads "positions refine under `P 42/m n m`, index 2 over the file's own `P n n m`; moments under the file's magnetic group", and `value` is that index (1 where the parent *is* the file's own group, e.g. a type-III group). At index > 1 the positions carry constraints the file's operators do not impose — right where the parent is the structure's real nuclear symmetry, a choice to check where a distortion accompanies the order: `Structure.from_cif(..., nuclear_group="file")` refines them under the file's own group instead |
| `CIF_MAGNETIC_NUCLEAR_SETTING` | (info — same reader, same channel) Assume `Phase.space_group` is the parent symbol the file names. It is not: the message reads "positions refine under `I b m 2`, the file's own group … and not under the parent `I m a 2` it names, because …; moments under the file's magnetic group". The symbol names the type, and the setting is checked against the file's own magnetic operators with time reversal dropped (`crystallography.magcif.nuclear_operations`); the phase is built under the tabulated setting whose operations are *exactly* those. Three shapes reach here: a symbol tabulated at another origin or axis choice (measured on Ima2/space group 46, read as `I b m 2`); a symmetry-lowering magnetic group whose asymmetric unit lists one parent orbit as two sites — under the parent that orbit would be counted twice; and `nuclear_group="file"`, which asks for it. Either way the phase carries exactly the atoms the file states, so nothing needs fixing. A fourth shape: the file's own group is in a setting **no** tabulated symbol names (an origin or axis choice gemmi's table lacks). The message then reads "carried as its own list of N operations under `P -1 [unnamed in this cell]`": `Phase.symmetry_operations` is the group, the bracketed label only names the closest type, so do not look the label up as a symbol. `Structure.to_cif` carries the list; a symbol-only writer (`.inp`, `.pcr`, `.EXP`) refuses the phase by name. A group whose rotations are in no tabulated orientation (a two-fold along [110]) is refused by name (`MagCifError`): restate the file in a conventional setting |
| `CIF_MAGNETIC_MAGNITUDE_ESD_NOT_STORED` | (info — same reader, same channel) Assume a `Structure` read back from a refinement CIF carries the moment's uncertainty. It does not: the file states `_atom_site_moment.magnitude_su`, and the esd of a moment's **modulus** is a property of the fit that produced it rather than of the structure, so a `Structure` has nowhere to put it. rietx keeps it in `FitReport.magnetic[i].magnitude_esd`, and `write_refinement_cif` is what writes it back into that tag. `value` is the number the file stated, so nothing is lost — it is just not on the model |
| `CIF_MAGNETIC_SPHERICAL_ESD_NOT_STORED` | (info — same reader, same channel) Assume a moment with `stderr=None` on every component was not refined. Here it says only that the file stated the moment in the spherical form alone, with standard uncertainties, and the conversion (modulus, polar, azimuth) → crystal-axis components is nonlinear and singular on the pole, so those su are not propagated. The message quotes each item whose su was dropped. A crystal-axis row keeps its own su, and a Cartesian row's su is propagated through its linear conversion, so restating the row in either form keeps them |

### The TOPAS and FullProf moment readers

These fire at *import*, from `rietx.io.projects` — the same channel §7g's
`TOPAS_*`/`FULLPROF_*` families use, never on `result.diagnostics` — and are
grouped with the rest of the magnetic family here rather than left in
`references/diagnostics-projects.md` (issue #286), since the question they
answer is the same order-parameter one the rest of this file answers.

| code | what you must not assume, and what to do |
|---|---|
| `FULLPROF_MAGNETIC_PHASE_OMITTED` | (warning — from `rietx.io.projects.fullprof.to_structure(..., nuclear_only=True, diagnostics=[])`) Read the returned `Structure` as the model the `.pcr` describes. You **declared** the omission by passing `nuclear_only=True`, and this says how large what you dropped is: the phase's index and name, its `Jbt`/`Isy`, its site count, how many propagation vectors it carries, which moment columns are non-zero, and its own `R_Bragg` (also on `value`). Every intensity that phase contributed is now unmodelled, so Rwp, the remaining phases' scales and every weight fraction absorb it. Without `nuclear_only=True` the build **refuses** instead: a `Jbt = 1` phase is a *pure magnetic* phase — no nuclear structure factor, its own `Scale`, a magnetic form-factor label in the species column — and rietx's magnetic model is a moment on a site of a nuclear phase sharing one scale, so the file's decomposition has nowhere to land. Its symmetry is a magnetic *representation* besides, whose matrices need not be the Shubnikov axial action ε·det(R)·R an operator list is. The route that does work: restate the phase as a nuclear phase carrying moments, which is what a magCIF states and `Structure.from_cif` reads |
| `TOPAS_MAGNETIC_GROUP_READ` | (info — from `rietx.io.projects.topas.to_structure(..., diagnostics=[])`) Assume the magnetic group was yours to supply. It was the file's own: `mag_space_group` stated a BNS number (`62.448`, `1.1`) or an OG number, and that was resolved to the operators of its **standard setting** — the setting TOPAS reads it in too. Where the `str` stated no `space_group` (TOPAS's own examples and ISODISTORT's export often do not), the message also names the nuclear group the positions refine under: the magnetic operators with time reversal dropped, tier 2 of the magCIF rule above. `where` names `phases.i.magnetic_symmetry` and/or `phases.i.space_group`. If the file's coordinates are in another setting, pass `to_structure(magnetic_symmetry=...)` an operator list; a spec you pass outranks the file's number |
| `TOPAS_MOMENT_CONVENTION` | (info — same channel) Assume `mlx mly mlz` are μ_B. They are not: they are **fractional-basis** components, m = mlx·a + mly·b + mlz·c, so the stored crystal-axis moment is (mlx·\|a\|, mly·\|b\|, mlz·\|c\|) — a per-axis scale on every cell, never a rotation. That is the TOPAS Technical Reference § 13 (Fmagc = L·Fmag; `MM_CrystalAxis_Display` gives mxc = mlx·a), and a file's own `MM_CrystalAxis_Display` line is the number to check the stored components against. It is also **measured**: TOPAS 6's calculated magnetic intensities on a β = 115° cell match this reading pair by pair and in \|m\|, and match neither a crystal-axis nor a Cartesian reading |
| `TOPAS_MOMENT_ION_UNCHARGED` | (info — same channel) Assume the magnetic form factor is the one the chemistry implies. A site carrying a moment states its species with no charge (`occ Cr` rather than `occ Cr+3`), so the **neutral atom's** ⟨j₀⟩ was used, and ⟨j₀⟩ for `Cr` and `Cr3+` are different curves. Write the charge into the site's `occ` — the reader normalises `Cr+3` to `Cr3+`, which is a form-factor key — or set `Moment.ion` on the built structure. The `.inp` sibling of `CIF_MAGNETIC_ION_UNCHARGED` |
