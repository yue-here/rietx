# 6. Abstention is a result. Do not convert it into a number

Load it when something declined to answer — an abstention, a caveat, a failed gate, a `best_or_none()` that came back `None`.

*A reference file of the `rietx` skill. The body it belongs to is [`SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md); section numbers are the ones the body cites.*

The package's hardest rule is **never return a confident wrong singleton**.
Several places will decline to answer. When they do, that *is* the answer —
propagate it, do not paper over it.

| Signal | Meaning | Correct agent response |
|---|---|---|
| `report.abstained_reason` is set | The global maturity gate refused Layer 1: the model is too far from converged for linearisation to mean anything | **Branch on `abstained_kind` first (WP-1057).** `"immature"` / `"unreadable"`: fix the fit using Layer 0; do not read `attribution`. `"resolution_limited"`: the basis *explains* the failing regions (median local R² ≳ 0.9) but its edit directions are indistinguishable on merged peaks — this is a statement about the data's resolution, **not evidence the model is wrong**, and for phase-ID-grade work a legitimate stopping point (§4b). Either way the actions that survive abstention (WP-1054) are the model-free ones **plus the position-family pointer**: when most misfitting regions have offsets beyond the linearisation radius, `reindex_or_recheck_cell` leads the list with the calibration candidates in `alternatives` — the same signature comes from a wrong cell and a gross zero/displacement error, and the data has not chosen. An `add_impurity_phase` at its capped 0.3 with `reindex_or_recheck_cell` first among alternatives means every "unmatched" peak matches the position-error evidence (displaced pairs / residual lobes) — do not add a phase on it |
| `TextureAnalysis.caveat` is set | Strong unmatched observed peaks coexist with the texture detection, and the per-reflection extraction partitions un-modelled intensity onto its calculated neighbours — an impurity can manufacture exactly this signature | The detection measures the residual, not necessarily the specimen. Resolve the unmatched peaks first; the `refine_preferred_orientation` action is already capped below `add_impurity_phase`, and the axis/r/R² stay readable as evidence |
| `region.gates_passed is False` | This region's coefficients failed resolvability / validity-radius / significance | Read `region.gate_failures`; the coefficients are present for transparency only and must **not** be read as causes |
| A trend is reported non-separable | Two angular templates (e.g. size vs strain) are collinear over this range | Do not pick one. Extend the range or report both |
| `PAWLEY_OVERLAP_UNRESOLVED` | See the explanation. | Treat the group sum as the datum |
| `STEPHENS_STRAIN_NOT_POSITIVE` | Fires under `solver="trf"`, and in a joint fit, which builds no cone | The S_HKL are **not quotable**, and this is never evidence *of* anisotropy. In a single fit, re-run on the default `lm` driver, which enforces the cone directly (measured on brucite: 7 of 43 reflections outside it under `trf`, 0 of 43 under `lm`, at a *higher* Rwp). That makes the answer admissible, not measured: vary the start and quote the coefficients only if they survive it (the `lm` result carries `CONSTRAINT_ACTIVE` when the cone actually bound) |
| `TEXTURE_NOT_POSITIVE` | The guard samples the whole sphere; the default `lm` driver holds P ≥ 0 only at the reflections | The coefficients and J are **not quotable** as a pole density, and this is never evidence that the specimen is textured. Lower the order or hold the highest terms (Measured: a March-Dollase r = 0.4 specimen fitted at order 2 ended at C2,0 = 2.90 with P = −0.45 on (hk0) under `trf`, and at the face C2,0 = 2.00 with `CONSTRAINT_ACTIVE` under `lm`) |
| `ADP_NOT_POSITIVE_DEFINITE` | See the explanation. | Revert the site to isotropic `biso` |
| `RIGID_BODY_UNSUPPORTED` | `where` names the DOFs, one finding per body | Its value and every body atom's esd are **not measurements**. Hold those DOFs and refit, then look at why: a turn that moves only zero-occupancy atoms, or one about an axis the template is symmetric under (Hypothesis: WP-1805's synthetic body only) |
| `RIGID_BODY_NOT_INVARIANT`, `RIGID_BODY_IMAGE_OVERLAP` | The table refused a body on or near a special position. NOT_INVARIANT: an atom sits on a symmetry element the body's origin is not on (a turn would push it off and the next stage count its images twice), or a torsion moves an on-site atom, or the site is in rhombohedral axes. IMAGE_OVERLAP: an image of a body atom lands on the body, so the body straddles an element and the cell would hold part of it twice (#876: S 8, O 32 in a PbSO₄ cell of 4 and 16) | Never move the body off the element to get it to build: that is #876's doubled composition. List one atom per image and put the origin on the element they share, `body_from_atoms(phase, labels, name, origin=<label or point>)`; the table then frees only the turns that site allows (0, 1 or 3). Disordered across the element: give its atoms a `disorder_group` and an occupancy of 1/|G_s| (1/2 across a mirror or a centre); a place whose occupancies sum to 1 or less (the atom and every distinct image within the limit of it: 1/2 + 1/2 on a mirror, three thirds on a 3-fold axis) is not refused either, a sum over 1 is the double count. Rhombohedral axes: restate the phase in `:H`. Off its element an atom takes the general multiplicity (*International Tables* Vol. A, Wyckoff positions) |
| `RIGID_BODY_MISFIT` | `ref.check_rigid_bodies(data)` (not run inside `fit()`; needs a fitted `ref`) found that releasing a body into restrained free atoms beats fitting it as a body, by both Hamilton (p < 0.001) and ΔBIC > 10 at N_eff. A good pose does not mean the right molecule: a synthetic body with one bond 0.3 Å long converged at GOF 2.33 against 1.01 and raised no other code (Measured: #775, its C–C molecule, not the test fixture) | The body's geometry is **not a measurement**. `message` names the released bonds/angles that moved most, in restraint σ: fix the template there, never the threshold. Run this once before quoting a body's geometry or calling a solve solved |
| `ROUGHNESS_UNCONSTRAINED` | The refined correction depresses no modelled reflection by >1 % | Drop the block |
| `NO_PHASES` | The model carries **no phase at all** — the fit was refused before it started, and no result exists to read. A pattern-only project is a legal state (WP-1207): peak picking and indexing work over it, the parameter table holds the instrument and the background, and only refining is refused | **Do not retry the fit** — nothing about it will change, and this is not the engine failing. Get a cell: `rx.index_pattern(data=..., instrument=...)` over the pattern, then adopt a candidate (§7d closes that loop); or supply one directly, where a space-group symbol and the cell parameters that *setting* leaves free are enough for a Le Bail scaffold. The refusal is on the verb, not on the model, so the same model is fine to hold, edit and index |
| `STRAIN_UNUSUALLY_LARGE` | A phase's sample strain refined past 1.5 deg (the FWHM coefficient of tanθ, `lor_strain`'s own units; `gauss_strain` is a variance and is compared as its square root). `where` names the term, `value` is the width | **A number to check, not a failure**; this is the one code here that is not a refusal. If `BOUND_HIT` fired on the same path the width is held at the data-derived cap and is **not** a measurement. A finite `Parameter.max` is your claim and replaces the cap; the flag still fires |
| `SIZE_UNUSUALLY_SMALL` | A phase's sample size broadening refined to an apparent crystallite below 5 nm (from `lor_size`, or `sqrt(gauss_size)`, via Scherrer at the pattern's wavelength). `where` names the term, `value` is the size in nm | **A number to check, not a failure**, the size twin of `STRAIN_UNUSUALLY_LARGE`. Below roughly 2 nm (a handful of unit cells) the scattering leaves the regime a broadened Bragg profile can model at all: the pattern becomes the Debye interference function of a cluster, peak positions shift off the Bragg angles, and the honest tool is total scattering/PDF, so a "size" refined down there no longer means size. If `BOUND_HIT` fired on the same path the width is held at the fitted-range cap and is **not** a measurement. A finite `Parameter.max` on the size term is your claim and replaces the cap; the flag still fires |
| `PHASE_UNCONSTRAINED` | This phase's scale is under the plan's `phase_support_sigma` (3σ by default) from zero, its esd taken against the counting noise with the phase's cell and widths free and the other phases' scales held. Visible calculated peaks do not contradict it: a scale fitted to noise draws them. Two phases that can draw one pattern are not flagged; `HIGH_CORRELATION` on their scales says the data cannot pick between them (Measured: #219's Cu ridge, WP-1523). `where` lists the parameters the hold acted on and the message says what the run did with them: **held** for the stages it names (the usual case since v1.3), or refined against it anyway. A third wording, `no reflection of phase N lies in the fitted range`, is the limit case: the range covers no line of the phase, so its scale is held too, it has no weight fraction (`None`, listed in `qpa.unmeasured_phases`), and the other fractions are relative to the phases seen in range, not to the specimen | **Those values are not measurements** either way, whatever their esds say. Do not read a converged status or a good Rwp as covering them: a parameter that does not move y_calc does not move Rwp either. Decide which case it is: the phase is not in this specimen and belongs out of the model; its scale is stuck at its floor and must be seeded before anything else of it is freed; or, for the third wording, the fitted range is wrong for the question. **Nothing is owed in response to the hold itself**: it releases itself in the stage where the phase appears, and re-running to "free" the parameters would only reproduce it. `RefinementPlan.phase_support_sigma` sets the threshold and `None` switches the hold off, except for a phase with no line in range; the message then says the parameters refined without signal. **A hold acts on a column, so `where` need not be a phase path**: if you tied that cell to a variable of your own, your `vars.X` is what stopped moving and is what `where` names. `StageResult.held_reach` maps it back to the parameters it was driving. A variable driving *two* phases is held only while the data can see neither of them; one visible phase gives it gradient again, in that stage or the next. On a series, expect this per pattern — see §7's note on reading it across a run |
| `SCALE_B_INSEPARABLE` | `where` is the held B; the message gives the weight-fraction change per Å² | Quote the phase's weight fraction **as conditional on that B**: its esd carries none of B's uncertainty. Supply B from elsewhere if the fraction matters, or widen the range to a second d-spacing, which lifts the hold |
| `BACKGROUND_ORDER_AT_CAP` | (info — on `select_chebyshev_order(...).diagnostics`, and a `UserWarning` from `auto_background(kind="chebyshev")`; not on `result.diagnostics`) The order scan returned `max_order` with BIC still falling and Durbin-Watson d short of the stop. `value` is the last d | Do not raise `max_order` to chase it: profile misfit leaks into the masked channels and keeps d low at every order (Measured: #833, 11-BM Si 640c returns 16 of 16 and 32 of 32; the cap biased the background by ≤ 0.7 % at the strongest peaks). The cap is common on a short lab scan: the worked default's own FAP pattern reaches it (16 of 16, d = 0.721), so expect this warning there. Read the fit's `BACKGROUND_ABSORPTION` first; switch to `kind="pspline"` only if that fires, and otherwise keep the order |
| `SEQUENTIAL_PATH_DEPENDENT` | **Judged per pattern, and only where both chains measured an esd for that same pattern** — so a parameter one chain held throughout, and one the two chains share no measured pattern for, is not judged at all | That trajectory is an artefact of the refinement order, not a measurement. Hold the parameter, restrain it, or quote the forward/backward spread as its uncertainty. **Silence is not clearance**: this code's absence means the two chains agreed *where they were comparable*, which for a path one direction held throughout is nowhere. **Two lengths cannot tell you which it was** — equal lengths are consistent with zero shared patterns, and identical labels with not one pattern measured by both. Count the patterns actually judged: intersect `series.trajectory(path).labels` with `series.backward.trajectory(path).labels` (`series.backward` is the reverse chain's own `SeriesResult`, set only when `direction="both"`), and keep those whose `arrays()` esd is finite in **both**. `0` patterns judged is "never examined"; a small count against a long series is "examined on a fraction of it". `tests/test_sequential.py` is this recipe, run on both of those shapes. Since WP-1333 the "never examined" case is also said: `SEQUENTIAL_PATH_CHECK_INCOMPLETE` at `info` names every path some chain measured that no pattern could judge, and at `warning` says the comparison did not run at all — the recipe is still how you count "a fraction of it" |
| `SEQUENTIAL_PERSISTENT_FINDING` | A per-pattern code fired in **more than half** the patterns; `where` names the parameter and `value` is the fraction | Change the model or the plan: refitting the individual patterns will reproduce it. One `BOUND_HIT` is a pattern that hit a bound; a `BOUND_HIT` in most of them is a bound that is wrong. In the episode this comes from, 425 `BOUND_HIT`s went unread for two hours while `phases.3.cell.c` was pinned in 42 of 68 patterns. The per-entry diagnostics still carry every occurrence. A code about how a pattern was measured rather than about the model (`FROZEN_COMPILE_STALE`, `sequential.NOT_A_SERIES_FINDING`) is never aggregated |
| `SEQUENTIAL_CANCELLED` | See the explanation. | The trajectory is **truncated, not finished**: do not read its last point as the end of the ramp, and do not compare a slope over it with one over the whole series |
| `SEQUENTIAL_UNRECOVERED` | A pattern diverged and stayed diverged after every rung of the escalation ladder (`entry.rungs_tried` names them) | Read that point as a **failed fit, not a datum**: do not interpolate across it and do not quote its parameters. The chain stepped over it, so the successor warm-started from the last pattern that converged and the reseed median never saw it. Fit the pattern on its own to find out why (a specimen change the model lacks, a bad scan, a starting model that no longer suits this end of the series) |
| `IndexingResult.best_or_none()` returns `None` | No candidate cell reached the confidence gate | **This is the most likely outcome of a first indexing run, and it is not a failure.** Read each candidate's `confidence_caveats` and act on the *refuting* ones first (§7c). Never take `candidates[0]` because it is ranked first — the ranking orders the hypotheses, the gate judges them, and the two are different questions. The order leads with **corroboration** (at least two engines found the lattice) and ranks the panel within that (WP-1046), so `candidates[0]` is "corroborated, then best on the panel" — closer to the gate's own reading, and still not it |
| `INDEX_ABSTAINED` | See the explanation. | Propagate the abstention. The candidates are there so you can see what was considered, not so you can pick one |
| `INDEX_GEOMETRIC_AMBIGUITY` | Two distinct lattices explain the positions equally well (Mighell & Santoro 1975) | Do not pick one. Collect to the 2θ in `discriminating_two_theta` and look for the reflections named there |
| `INDEX_MULTIPLE_SOLUTIONS` | See the explanation. | Compare the panels, and each `lebail.rwp` where neither cell predicts every reflection of the other (`supercell_checks` says; such a cell fits at least as well), and extend the 2θ range: two cells that both explain a range this wide are usually separated by one high-angle reflection |
| `INDEX_SEARCH_INCOMPLETE` | The per-system budget, a size cap on the grid or trial set, or the run's ceiling stopped a system before its domain was exhausted | Only a *completed* exhaustive search says "no cell exists", and `search_complete[system]` says which systems finished. The message names the cause and the suggestion its remedy: `budget_seconds` for the first, a narrower search for a cap, `total_budget_seconds` for the ceiling. A budget cut also moves the *order*: two runs of one brucite search cut at 60 s put different cells first, so do not compare rankings across runs unless both finished (Measured: WP-1449) |
| `INDEX_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` | The declared whole-run ceiling (`total_budget_seconds`) bound | Read the three states of `systems_searched` (§7c) before treating any absence — of a system, a candidate, or a validation — as evidence. Read `engines_run` too, because the engines take turns within a system and the first can spend the whole ceiling. On a 224 Å³ monoclinic cell over the default axis range, dichotomy returns the cell 8 s of CPU into the search with `rietx-kernels` ≥ 1.3, and inside 120 s on its numpy loop; before 1.9 a 120 s ceiling returned nothing and svd never started. For a monoclinic or hexagonal search with a large volume bound, set `max_volume` from the formula (`SearchSpec.from_formula`), keep `max_d_axis` as low as you can justify (the grid cost 10× more CPU at 17 Å than at 11 Å), search one system per call, and give the ceiling room for the grid (Measured: WP-1924, WP-1959, one synthetic 61-line pattern) |
| `INDEX_INLIER_RETRY` | No lattice two engines agree on came back while a cell could leave two search lines unindexed, so the run was searched again accepting a cell that indexes 60 % of them and the five strongest | Read the answer as found under that second rule. Read each candidate's `n_search_unindexed` and `unindexed_two_theta`: a cell leaving the weak lines is the phase, and its residue an impurity, a halo or picker junk |
| `INDEX_SYSTEMS_NOT_COVERED` | See the explanation. | Never read a failure as "this is multiphase" (§8.15) |
| `EXTINCTION_SCREEN_UNRELIABLE` | The screen's reference fit (the absence-free class) leaves more than half of the net signal's weighted power unexplained (`reference_rwp_net`), so every refutation and ΔBIC below it may read misfit, not absences; `status` is `"unreliable"` and `best_or_none()` is `None` | Do not pick from the ranked classes. See the explanation for what to check before re-screening |
| `ExtinctionScreen.best_or_none()` returns `None` | No extinction class is separated from its rivals by these data | Read the ranked `candidates` and `EXTINCTION_SYMBOL_AMBIGUOUS`, which states every reason at once. The action is a longer count at the *forbidden positions the leading classes disagree about*, or a wider range — never the better Rwp, which always favours the class with more reflections |
| `EXTINCTION_GROUPS_NOT_SEPARABLE` | See the explanation. | Carry the whole list forward and choose with chemistry (§7e) |
| `EXTINCTION_PRIOR_APPLIED` | A declared `restrict=` (`ExtinctionPrior`: `sohncke`, `centrosymmetric`, `polar`, and `z` or `density` with `formula`, `ordered`) removed members from classes; `where` names each with the fact it fails, the special positions a kept member needs at Z′ < 1, and a kept member whose orbits cannot hold the declared count (removed only under `ordered=True`). `warning` when it emptied the class the data rank first, or when the density does not give the declared Z on this cell | It is a filter, never evidence: no ΔBIC moved. On `info`, carry the narrowed `space_groups`. On `warning`, check the declaration before the answer (a racemate declared enantiopure, a twinned polar sample); `best_or_none()` is `None` when the data prefer an excluded class decisively |
| `LAUE_CLASS_NOT_SEPARABLE` | The leading class's members fall in more than one Laue class (`laue_classes`); they differ only in which reflections are equivalent, never in a position or an absence | Do not pick a Laue class from these data. Run the structure solution once per Laue class, not once per group |

`Layer 1`'s gates, for reference, are: resolvability on the **scale-normalised**
Gram matrix, a 0.4·FWHM validity radius (a peak 5 FWHM off must trigger
"re-detect", never a confident small-offset reading), local χ²_red
significance, and share-based global maturity. Confidence weights *importance*
(share of χ²), not only statistical significance — at high counting statistics
the second-order leakage of a peak shift into the width column is significant
but carries a per-cent-level share.

The per-region refusals arrive typed — `region.gate_failures` is a list of
`GateFailure(code, message)` since WP-1003, promoted from formatted strings
precisely so a consumer can branch on the name; the `message` carries the
measured numbers and is display-only. The vocabulary (`GateCode`) is closed,
the global maturity gate has no entry here (it abstains the whole layer,
`abstained_reason`/`abstained_kind` above), and one member is not a failure at
all:

| `GateFailure.code` | Meaning | Correct response |
|---|---|---|
| `no_significant_misfit` | this region's local χ²_red is at the noise floor — there is nothing to attribute | Nothing. This is a region the model already fits, **not** a failure of the basis and not evidence about the model; a report full of these is what a good fit looks like |
| `local_r2` | the region's misfit is real but the basis explained too little of it (low local R²) | Do not read the coefficients as causes. The misfit is not position/width/intensity/mixing/asymmetry-shaped — suspect what the basis cannot express: an unmatched peak (Layer 0), background shape, peak-shape misfit |
| `gram_condition` | the templates' scale-normalised Gram matrix is ill-conditioned here: the edit directions are indistinguishable on these merged peaks | The resolution-limited signature, per region (`abstained_kind="resolution_limited"` is the same statement globally). Do not pick one coefficient — the separation is absent from the data at this resolution, so the fix is a wider range or better resolution, never a preference |
| `outside_validity_radius` | the fitted offset exceeds the 0.4·FWHM linearisation radius, and a linear fit pushed past it *saturates* | **Re-detect, never shift**: read the fitted offset as a lower bound, not a measurement. Widespread across misfitting regions, this is exactly what emits `reindex_or_recheck_cell` with the calibration candidates in `alternatives` (the abstention table's first row, above) |
