Six folders, five patterns. NaCoO2/ holds a TOPAS input whose xdd line names a file that is not in the repository — but it writes Out_CIF_STR("KD1-2_riet_01.cif"), so it is the refinement script for the KD1-2_5 pattern, and that is how it was used.

How the missing information was filled in

Three of the five patterns arrive with no instrument description. All three sit on a bit-identical 2θ grid — 5.01436° to 89.99116° in 5086 points — as FeSb_19RBM.xy, whose .inp does describe the diffractometer. That fixes Cu Kα with the doublet weights the file pins (Kα2/Kα1 = 0.5295, held), LP_Factor(90) → a constant polarisation factor, and Simple_Axial_Model(!axial, 10) → axial divergence held, not refined.

Two protocol choices had to depart from the TOPAS inputs, both because the inputs rely on a calibration file that is not in the repository:

  • Instrument ⊕ sample width split. TOPAS refines only sample terms (CS_L, Strain_G) against a frozen instrument function. With no line-profile standard the split is unidentifiable — freeing both gives ρ = 1.000 between instrument.profile.w and phases.0.gauss_size. So the instrument Caglioti set carries the total width and the phase terms stay at zero. Same physics, opposite bookkeeping.
  • Tied displacement parameters. The FeSb input ties one total_beq across all three sites. pxrd-refine has no user-level equality tie, so it was emulated by a fixed-point loop (refine one B, copy it onto the others, refit) — converged in 7 cycles.

The one real disagreement is Biso, and it is the parameter you would expect: neither model carries a flat-plate absorption or surface-roughness term, so whatever low-angle intensity deficit exists is shared out between scale, background and B. MnSb_34_impure — naming the impurity

MnSb + Sb

examples/MnSb_34_impure/ · 4488 pts, 15–90° two phases, quantified

Given

A bare two-column .xy and the word "impure" in the folder name.

Inferred

Host indexed from the pattern: d(110) = 2.0704 → a = 4.1408, d(002) = 2.8931 → c = 5.7862, which then predicts (101) at 3.048 and (102) at 2.2517 against observed 3.0495 and 2.2518. Impurity from its two leftover lines, d = 3.114 and 2.154 — rhombohedral Sb (A7, R‑3m) puts (012) at 3.111 and (110) at 2.154. The sibling synchrotron .inp carries a commented-out phase_name Sb block in space group 166: the same identification, made by whoever collected the data.

Refined Sb cell against the literature A7 values (4.3084, 11.2740 Å): +0.06 % in a, +0.5 % in c, from a phase carrying 1.7 % of the sample. Dropping the Sb phase costs 0.52 points of Rwp (5.39 → 5.91 %), which is what a genuine minor phase looks like.

The host's lines are anisotropically broadened — hk0 much broader than 00l — so a Stephens block was added. Under the default trust-region driver the coefficients leave the physical σ²(M) ≥ 0 cone and the guard fires. Run under solver="lm", which carries the cone as a linear inequality, the fit is better (5.39 % vs 5.51 %) and the guard falls silent: the anisotropy here is real Stephens-type strain.

The pattern is one enormous (002) at 16.15° and about a dozen weak reflections — a strongly aligned layered platelet, which the refined r = 0.50 states quantitatively. Rwp is 3.07 %; the input's own header records r_wp 2.909 for whatever run produced it.

The input declares the two Na site occupancies and all four Biso as refinable. Neither survives this data:

  • Biso — freeing all four from four different starting values gives four different answers (0.0 → stays 0.0; 0.27 → 0.28–0.33; 1.0 → 3.2–9.9; 3.0 → stays 3.0) while Rwp moves by 0.16 points. That is a flat direction, not a measurement. Held at the input's values.
  • Na occupancies — they do refine, to 0.129(77) and 0.465(85), total Na 0.59 against the input's held 0.74, for 0.09 points of Rwp. A 20 % change in composition bought by a change smaller than the background-order sensitivity below is not a composition measurement.

Background order is worth naming because it moves the answer: 6 terms (as the input uses) gives a = 2.83223, 10 gives 2.83224, 15 (the automatic choice) gives 2.83254. The 6-term value lands within 1 ppm of the input's 2.832224.

The structural answers agree — the interstitial occupancy to 0.02 σ, and pxrd-refine's own µR estimate from composition and geometry lands within 0.3 % of the number the input computes from its mass-attenuation call. The profile does not agree, and the reason is visible rather than statistical.

Three things this pattern exercised that the lab patterns did not, each reported as what it changed rather than as an Rwp movement:

  • Rouse cylindrical absorption at µR = 1.273 removes a Biso bias of +0.2225 Å². Switching it off moves Biso(Sb) from −0.58 to −0.80 — which is the whole content of the correction, since it is exactly a reparameterisation of {scale, B}. pxrd-refine also flags that µR = 1.27 is outside the range Rouse et al. fitted (µR ≤ 1), so the transmission factor is an extrapolation. The input's Cylindrical_I_Correction is subject to the same limit and does not say so.
  • Stephens anisotropic strain improves Rwp from 17.1 % to 12.5 % — but the coefficients leave the physical cone on 19 of 58 reflections. Re-run under bounded LM, with the cone carried as a linear inequality, almost the whole improvement disappears (16.6 %). The width anisotropy in this pattern is not Stephens-type strain. The identical test on MnSb_34 gives the opposite verdict, which is what makes it a test.
  • A true Voigt peak shape instead of TCHZ pseudo-Voigt: 12.45 % vs 12.47 %. Not the problem.

HL2-1 — not identifiable from what is supplied

Unknown; not cubic, tetragonal or hexagonal

examples/HL2-1/HL2-1_2.xy · 5086 pts, 5–90°, Cu Kα no structure to refine

Given

A two-column pattern. No input, no formula, no sibling folder, nothing in the repository's history or source that names it.

Inferred

The diffractometer, from the bit-identical 2θ grid. Nothing else — three attempts are recorded below, all negative.

74 resolvable reflections between d = 7.92 and 1.095 Å. Three independent attempts at an identification:

  • Pair-seeded autoindexing over cubic, tetragonal and hexagonal metrics. The best solution indexes 68 % of the observed peak intensity. The identical code recovers MnSb_34's true cell (a = 4.1417, c = 5.7803 Å) at 82 %, so 68 % is a failure and not a threshold effect.
  • A 36-phase screen over the chemistry the rest of the collection is made of — Fe, Mn, Co, Na, Sb and their oxides and antimonides — plus everyday laboratory phases. Nothing scores convincingly; the leaders are simply the candidates predicting the most lines.
  • The same screen over pairs and triples. The best three-phase combination indexes 67 % of the intensity using 104 predicted lines against 74 observed peaks. Chance level.

A Pawley fit on the best-scoring hexagonal metric settles that cell quantitatively: 296 free reflection intensities, and Rwp is still 10.1 % with entire observed peaks missed (16.6°, 33.3°, 34.0°, 55.9°). A correct cell in Pawley mode fits to the noise. This one does not.

Corrected after publication: this card originally read “at least two phases”. That does not follow. Feeding the indexer complete synthetic peak lists from single-phase orthorhombic and monoclinic compounds scores them at 50–60 % — the same signature — against 82–100 % for genuinely tetragonal or hexagonal ones. HL2-1's 69 % rules out cubic, tetragonal and hexagonal, and does not distinguish a mixture from one low-symmetry phase. Measured in the companion provenance audit.

So the deliverable for HL2-1 is the extracted peak table — 2θ, d and relative intensity for all 74 lines — which is what a database search actually consumes.

What carried across

  • The independent check held. On the one dataset with a converged reference, an independently written Rietveld code lands within 100 ppm on the cell, inside 1 σ on the refined occupancy, and within 0.02 points on Rwp, Rexp and Durbin–Watson. The single disagreement — Biso — is traceable to a background and absorption modelling difference, not to the fit.
  • Rwp was the wrong judge three times out of five. The capillary absorption correction provably cannot move it (it is a reparameterisation of scale and B) and its whole content is a 0.22 Å² B bias. The Stephens block improves it in both MnSb samples, and in one of them the improvement is entirely unphysical. Every claim here is anchored to a record field or a guard, not to a fit index.
  • The two guards that changed conclusions were the Stephens positivity cone — which distinguished a real anisotropy (MnSb_34) from a fitted one (MnSb_33) with the same block and the same seed — and the start-dependence check on KD1-2_5's Biso, which turned four refined numbers into four held ones.
  • Nothing was invented for HL2-1. The alternative to a phase assignment is a peak table, not a plausible-looking fit. One inference about it was overstated — see the correction above, and the companion audit of the non-refinement tooling, which also measures what the assumed goniometer radius costs the cell parameters quoted here (roughly ±85 ppm, larger than their esds).
